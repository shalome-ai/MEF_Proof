# Rosetta stone: mathematical jargon, a first-year translation, and plain English

**What this is.** A working aid for the PI and Claude to understand each other. First drafted by the PI; reviewed and edited by Claude on 12 November 2026.

**How it is used.** Claude answers as it normally does. When the PI writes **"Please Rosetta Translate x"**, Claude re-states x in three parts, without changing what x says: first **"In a nutshell"**, at most four sentences saying what x claims and what it asks of the PI; then the terms, in the three columns below; then x sentence by sentence in plain English. The nutshell is a summary for orientation only: nothing is argued from it and it is entered nowhere.

**How it grows (CLAUDE.md §3).** When a term of jargon is used with the PI and is not here, Claude adds a row to Part B in that session and says so in its end-of-session report.

**What it is not.**
- It is not a source. The entries are written from memory and are not checked against a text. Nothing here is cited, and nothing here enters a paper, a register or a premise (CLAUDE.md §4).
- It is not the Definitions Register. Where M1 gives a word its own meaning (minimal, clause (a), modulus, drive, step, shadow, state at rest), the Definitions Register and the paper govern, not this file.
- The third column is an intuition. It is deliberately loose and is never to be argued from.

**Part A** is the PI's list, edited. **Part B** adds standard terms that M1 and the discussion of J2 use and Part A did not have.

## Part A

| The Pure Math Jargon | Your Freshman Translation | The Plain English Intuition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A-genus (A-hat genus)** | A specific rational number calculated from the Pontryagin classes of a manifold. On a spin manifold it is an integer, the index of the Dirac operator. | A fingerprint of the shape's twisting. On a shape that carries spinors it counts, net, the spinor waves at rest; if it is not zero the shape cannot be curved positively everywhere. (Whether a shape can carry spinors at all is a different test: see *Spin manifold*.) |
| **Abelian surface** | A 2-dimensional complex torus that can be embedded in projective space. | A 4D donut whose points can be added like numbers, and the order of adding never matters. |
| **Absolutely minimal models** | A smooth surface with no (−1)-curves (curves that can be blown down without creating singularities), and the only such surface in its class. | A surface stripped down to its most basic shape, with no extra "fluff" attached, and only one way of doing the stripping. |
| **Adjoint representation** | The natural way a Lie group acts on its own Lie algebra by conjugation ($gXg^{-1}$). | How a group of symmetries transforms its own infinitesimal building blocks. |
| **Affine varieties** | The set of common zeros of a system of polynomial equations in $k^n$. | A geometric shape carved out by polynomial equations in a flat coordinate system. |
| **Algebraic curves** | 1-dimensional complex varieties (which look like 2D surfaces over the reals). | A curve (a 2D surface to the eye) defined by polynomial equations rather than by calculus. It may have a few sharp points. |
| **Almost complex structure** | A smooth linear map $J$ on the tangent bundle such that $J^2 = -1$. | A rule that turns every direction by a quarter-turn, so that a real space can be treated, point by point, as if made of complex numbers. |
| **Anosov diffeomorphism** | A diffeomorphism of a compact manifold whose tangent space splits everywhere, in a way the map preserves, into a uniformly expanding and a uniformly contracting direction. | A perfect taffy-puller map: stretching the space in one direction while squeezing it in another, at every point without exception, forever. |
| **Betti numbers** | The ranks of the homology groups of a topological space. | A count of how many separate pieces, 1D loops-round-holes, 2D hollow cavities, and so on, a shape has. |
| **Bigraded cohomology groups** | Cohomology groups indexed by two integers, usually tracking holomorphic and anti-holomorphic forms ($H^{p,q}$). | Sorting the topological holes of a space into a two-dimensional grid based on complex coordinates. |
| **Bimeromorphic** | A map between complex manifolds that is an isomorphism away from lower-dimensional analytic subsets on each side. | Two shapes that are identical once you ignore a few zero-thickness scratches. |
| **Blowing up / Blowing down** | Replacing a point with the projective space of all directions through it (up), or collapsing that space back into a point (down). | Up: pull a point apart into "one point for each direction through it", so that curves that crossed there no longer touch. Down: squash that set of directions back to a single point. |
| **Bochner's vanishing theorem** | A theorem stating that certain harmonic forms (or spinors) must be zero when a curvature is positive; the first case: positive Ricci curvature leaves no harmonic 1-forms. | Proof that if a shape curves tightly enough, it cannot support certain waves at rest. |
| **Bundle of exterior forms** | The vector bundle whose sections are differential forms (wedge products of cotangent vectors). | A backpack attached to every point containing all the measuring tapes for length, area and volume. |
| **Canonical bundle** | The top exterior power of the holomorphic cotangent bundle of a complex manifold. | The master volume-measuring tool for a complex shape. |
| **Cartan matrix** | The matrix of integers $2(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)/(\alpha_j,\alpha_j)$ formed from the simple roots of a Lie algebra. | The blueprint that completely defines a continuous system of symmetries: a small table of whole numbers recording the angles between its basic directions. |
| **Chern class** | Characteristic classes of complex vector bundles that measure topological non-triviality. | A barcode that records how a complex space attached to a shape is twisted; for a line, a whole number of twists. |
| **Chern-Simons invariant** | A number attached to a connection on a 3-manifold: the integral of the Chern–Simons 3-form, defined up to whole numbers. | A subtle measurement of how much a field twists, links and knots around itself over a three-dimensional shape. |
| **Clifford algebra** | An algebra generated by a vector space equipped with a quadratic form, where $v^2 = -\lVert v\rVert^2$. | The machinery that generalises the $i, j, k$ of the quaternions to any number of dimensions. Spinors are what it acts on. |
| **Coboundary** | An element in the image of the differential operator $d$ in a cochain complex. | Something that is the "derivative" of something else. Such things are counted as trivial when holes are counted. |
| **Cochain mapping** | A homomorphism between cochain complexes that commutes with the differential. | A translation dictionary between two different ways of calculating a space's holes, keeping all rules intact. |
| **Codimension one** | A subspace with dimension exactly one less than the ambient total space. For an Anosov diffeomorphism ("of codimension 1"): the expanding or the contracting direction is a single line. | A wall in a room, or the skin of an apple: just one dimension thinner than the whole thing. |
| **Coherent algebraic sheaves** | Sheaves of modules over the structure sheaf that are locally finitely generated and presented. | A well-behaved family of algebraic functions patched together over a shape. |
| **Complex manifold** | A space that locally looks like $\mathbb{C}^n$ with holomorphic transition functions. | A multi-dimensional space where you can do complex calculus everywhere smoothly. |
| **Conformal invariance** | Unchanged under metric scaling (multiplying the metric by a strictly positive function). | A property that doesn't care if you zoom in or zoom out, differently at different places, as long as the angles stay the same. |
| **Connection matrix** | A matrix of 1-forms specifying how basis vectors twist when parallel transported. | The steering instructions telling you how to carry a direction "straight" across a curved surface. |
| **Continuous splitting** | A continuous decomposition of a vector bundle into a direct sum of subbundles. | Slicing the available directions you can move in into distinct, continuously varying lanes. |
| **Covariant derivative** | A way to take directional derivatives of sections of a vector bundle along a manifold. | How to measure the rate of change of a wind current while you're walking on a bumpy hill. |
| **Cup-product** | A product in cohomology that multiplies a class of degree $p$ and $q$ to get one of degree $p+q$. | A way of multiplying two kinds of hole; it mirrors what happens when the things wrapped round them cross each other. |
| **D+d* operator** | The Hodge-de Rham operator, combining the exterior derivative and its adjoint. | The master calculus operator that does gradient, divergence and curl all at once on curved spaces. |
| **Diffeomorphism** | A smooth bijection with a smooth inverse. | A perfect, smooth morphing of a rubber sheet without creasing, tearing or gluing. (Compare *Homeomorphism* in Part B, where creases are allowed.) |
| **Dirac operator** | A first-order differential operator acting on spinors whose square is the Laplacian, up to a curvature term. | The quantum version of the gradient/divergence operator, tracking how spinning particles change from place to place. |
| **Dolbeault complex** | The complex analog of the de Rham complex using the $\bar{\partial}$ operator. | A measuring system that sorts functions and forms on a complex shape by how far they are from being complex-smooth. |
| **Double complexes** | A grid of modules with vertical and horizontal differentials, each of which applied twice gives zero, and which anti-commute. | A 2D spreadsheet of algebra where differentiating twice down, or twice across, always gives zero, and down-then-across is minus across-then-down. |
| **Double covering** | A 2-to-1 continuous map where a space wraps around a base space twice. | Wrapping a shape around another shape exactly twice, like a rubber band looped twice around your finger. |
| **Dynkin diagrams** | A graph where nodes are simple roots and edges indicate the angle between them. | A stick-figure drawing; the full list of such drawings classifies the basic continuous symmetry systems (the simple Lie algebras). |
| **Einstein manifold** | A Riemannian manifold whose Ricci tensor is proportional to the metric. | A shape where the (Ricci) curvature is evenly distributed everywhere. |
| **Elliptic curve** | A smooth projective curve of genus 1 with a specified base point. | A donut-shaped surface defined by a cubic equation, where you can "add" points together like numbers. |
| **Elliptic differential operator** | An operator whose symbol is invertible in every non-zero direction; its solutions are as smooth as the data allow. | An equation of equilibrium or diffusion type, which smooths out any rough edges in its solutions. |
| **Enriques surfaces** | An algebraic surface with $q=0$, $p_g=0$ and a canonical bundle of order 2. | A 4D shape that is exactly half of a K-3 surface, got by folding the K-3 surface onto itself with no point left fixed. |
| **Ergodic** | A dynamical system with an invariant measure in which every invariant set has measure zero or full measure; time averages then equal space averages. | A system that scrambles thoroughly: a typical starting point wanders over the whole space, spending time in each region in proportion to its size. |
| **Exact sequence** | A sequence of homomorphisms where the image of one is the kernel of the next. | A perfectly efficient assembly line: everything produced by one machine is exactly what the next one consumes. |
| **Exotic spheres** | Manifolds homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to the standard $n$-sphere. | A shape that is a perfect sphere to the touch, but on which calculus is done in a genuinely different way. |
| **Exterior product** | The wedge product ($\wedge$), which is associative and alternating ($v \wedge v = 0$). | The maths of building areas out of lines and volumes out of areas, where parallel lines span zero area. |
| **Foliations (Feuilletages)** | A decomposition of an $n$-manifold into disjoint connected immersed $k$-dimensional submanifolds (the leaves), locally stacked like parallel planes. | Slicing a solid shape into infinitely many infinitely thin, non-intersecting layers, like a stack of paper. |
| **Fundamental group** | The group of equivalence classes of loops under homotopy ($\pi_1$). | The record of the distinct ways you can loop a lasso around the holes of a shape. |
| **Fundamental weights** | The basis of the weight lattice of a Lie algebra dual to the simple coroots. | The fundamental "measuring units" for the quantum numbers of symmetrical particles. |
| **Geometric genus** | The dimension of the space of global holomorphic $n$-forms on an $n$-manifold ($p_g$). | A count of how many independent, complex-smooth, volume-measuring fields fit on a shape. |
| **Harmonic forms** | Differential forms annihilated by the Hodge Laplacian $\Delta = dd^* + d^*d$. | A field or wave on a surface that has reached a state of perfect, lowest-energy rest. |
| **Harmonic spinors** | Spinor fields in the kernel of the Dirac operator. | The balanced, at-rest state of a quantum spinning particle on a curved surface. |
| **Hermitian line bundle** | A complex line bundle equipped with a smoothly varying Hermitian inner product. | A complex 1D space attached everywhere on a shape, coming with a built-in ruler to measure length. |
| **Hodge structures** | A decomposition of a complexified lattice into subspaces $H^{p,q}$ satisfying $\overline{H^{p,q}} = H^{q,p}$. | Sorting the topological holes of a shape into bins according to how they sit relative to the complex coordinates. |
| **Hodge's theorem** | On a compact Riemannian manifold every de Rham cohomology class contains exactly one harmonic representative. | For every type of hole in a closed shape, there is exactly one perfectly balanced wave that wraps around it. |
| **Holomorphic cohomology** | Cohomology with coefficients in the sheaf of holomorphic functions (or holomorphic sections of a bundle). | Measuring the obstructions to patching together complex-smooth functions across a shape. |
| **Holomorphic square roots** | A line bundle $L$ such that $L \otimes L$ is isomorphic to a given bundle. | A halfway point: a bundle that, multiplied by itself, gives the bundle you started with (for spinors, the master volume bundle). |
| **Holonomy** | The transformation a vector undergoes when parallel transported around a closed loop. | The record of how much a vector got twisted after a walking tour around a loop. |
| **Homological argument** | Using exact sequences, diagram chasing, and functor properties to prove theorems. | Proving a geometric fact not by measuring the shape, but by tracking the bookkeeping of its holes. |
| **Homogeneous spaces** | A manifold equipped with a transitive Lie group action (e.g., $G/H$). | A perfectly uniform space where everywhere looks exactly the same as everywhere else. |
| **Hopf manifold** | A complex manifold defined as $\mathbb{C}^n \setminus \{0\} / \langle z \mapsto 2z \rangle$, $n \ge 2$. | A shape made by taking all of space (except the origin) and gluing each point to the point twice as far out. |
| **Hyperelliptic curves** | An algebraic curve of genus $g \ge 2$ that admits a 2-to-1 map to the projective line. | A multi-holed donut that is highly symmetrical, built by gluing two spheres together along a few slits. |
| **Injectively immersed cells** | The image of a cell (a copy of $\mathbb{R}^k$) under a smooth map that is one-to-one and has no creases. It may still wind densely through the space. | A flat patch laid into a shape smoothly, never folding over or crossing itself, though it may wind round and round for ever. |
| **Inner automorphism** | An automorphism of a group given by conjugation ($x \mapsto gxg^{-1}$). | A symmetry generated completely from inside the system. |
| **Invariant Lebesgue measure** | A measure given by a density against ordinary volume that a given map leaves unchanged. | A way to measure the "amount" of space that stays constant even as the system scrambles. |
| **Irregularity** | The dimension of the space of holomorphic 1-forms on a complex surface ($q = h^{0,1}$ in the Kähler case). | A count of the independent complex-smooth "length-measuring" fields on the shape: roughly, half the number of its independent loops. |
| **Irreducible representation** | A vector space acted on by a group with no non-trivial invariant subspaces. | The most basic, indivisible building block of a symmetry acting on something. |
| **Isotropy subgroup** | The subgroup of elements that fix a specific point in a group action (the stabilizer). | The set of moves you can still make while keeping one finger pinned to a single spot. |
| **Jordan-Brouwer separation theorem** | An embedding of $S^{n-1}$ into $\mathbb{R}^n$ separates it into exactly two components. | If you draw a closed balloon in any dimension, it separates an "inside" from an "outside". |
| **Kähler manifold** | A complex manifold with a Hermitian metric whose imaginary part is a closed 2-form. | The gold standard of spaces, where complex numbers, geometry and topology all cooperate. |
| **Kählerian K-3 surfaces** | A simply connected, compact complex surface with a trivial canonical bundle, carrying a Kähler metric. | A beautiful 4D complex shape with no loops that cannot be shrunk (it does have two-dimensional holes) and a perfectly balanced volume measure. |
| **Kervaire-Milnor group** | The group of exotic spheres under the operation of connected sum. | The catalogue of all the different ways calculus can be set up on a sphere of a given dimension. |
| **Kronecker product** | The tensor product of matrices. | Multiplying two grids of numbers together to get a large grid containing every possible product of their entries. |
| **Kuranishi family** | The locally complete family of deformations of a complex structure. | The control room that shows every possible way you can bend or tweak a complex shape a little. |
| **Lefschetz principle** | Any first-order sentence true in $\mathbb{C}$ is true in any algebraically closed field of characteristic 0. | A licence to borrow algebra proofs done with complex numbers and apply them to abstract number systems of the same kind. |
| **Lie algebra** | The tangent space at the identity of a Lie group, equipped with a bracket operation. | The study of "infinitesimal symmetries": what a rotation looks like if you only turn by a trillionth of a degree. |
| **Linearly disjoint** | Two subfields of a larger field whose bases don't algebraically interfere. | Two number systems inside a bigger one that are completely independent and share no overlapping information. |
| **Local product neighborhood** | A small region that looks exactly like a product of two pieces; for an Anosov map, (a piece of contracting sheet) × (a piece of expanding sheet). | A zoomed-in view in which the two families of layers cross like the threads of a woven cloth. |
| **Local rings** | A commutative ring with a unique maximal ideal. | The algebraic magnifying glass that focuses purely on the geometry at one single point. |
| **Maximal prime ideal** | An ideal not contained in any other proper ideal. | The largest chunk of a number system you can "set to zero" and still have a working system of numbers left. |
| **Modules** | A generalization of vector spaces over rings instead of fields. | Like a vector space, but the "scalars" you multiply by may have awkward rules (such as no fractions). |
| **Monodromy** | How a mathematical object fails to return to its original state after traveling around a singularity or a loop. | Walking in a circle around a strange point, only to find yourself changed, perhaps upside down, when you get back. |
| **Nakai-Moisezon criterion** | A numerical condition (positive intersection numbers with every subvariety) for a divisor on an algebraic variety to be ample. | A checklist to ensure a shape "bulges outward" properly in all directions. |
| **Non-wandering points** | Points in a dynamical system where every neighborhood eventually intersects its own forward iterations. | Places where something always comes back: however small a patch you draw round the point, some of it returns to the patch later. The point itself need not return. |
| **Null space** | The kernel of a linear operator. | The black hole of a matrix: everything that gets sent to zero. |
| **Open coverings** | A collection of open sets whose union contains the entire topological space. | A bunch of overlapping blankets thrown over a shape until no part is exposed. |
| **Open embedding** | A homeomorphism onto an open subset of the target space. | Pasting a small shape into a larger space so that it forms a clean, borderless region. |
| **Orthogonal Lie algebra** | The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{so}(n)$ of skew-symmetric matrices representing infinitesimal rotations. | The engine that generates every possible rotation in a space. |
| **P-simplex** | The $p$-dimensional generalization of a triangle or tetrahedron. | The simplest geometric building block for any dimension (a point, a line, a triangle, a pyramid). |
| **Parallel spinor** | A spinor field with zero covariant derivative everywhere. | A quantum spin state that stays rigidly locked in the same alignment no matter where you move. |
| **Period mapping** | A map assigning to each member of a family of complex manifolds its Hodge structure. | Translating the geometry of a shape into a list of integrals, to track how the shape changes as it is deformed. |
| **Pi_1-conjugate** | Two maps $f$, $g$ for which there is an isomorphism $\phi$ of fundamental groups with $\phi f_* = g_* \phi$. | Two maps that move lassos round in exactly matching patterns, once the lassos of one shape are relabelled as those of the other. |
| **Pluricanonical systems** | Linear systems associated with tensor powers of the canonical bundle. | A whole series of master volume-measuring tools used to classify the overall geometry of a shape. |
| **Poincare-Bendixon theory** | A theorem stating that a bounded trajectory of a smooth flow in the plane must settle onto fixed points or a closed cycle. | If you draw an endless, non-crossing path on a flat piece of paper without leaving a bounded region, it eventually has to spiral into a loop or stop. |
| **Pontrjagin forms** | Differential forms built from the curvature matrix that represent the Pontryagin classes of a real vector bundle. | Topological fingerprints that measure the twisting of real (not complex) geometry. |
| **Positive definite quadratic form** | A quadratic form $Q(x)$ such that $Q(x) > 0$ for all $x \ne 0$. | A bowl shape that curves upward in every single direction, never dipping to zero except at the bottom. |
| **Positive scalar curvature** | A condition where the volume of every small geodesic ball is less than the Euclidean volume. | A space that, averaged over all directions, curves inwards everywhere, like the surface of a ball. |
| **Primitive cohomology class** | A class in $H^k$ ($k \le n$) killed by $L^{n-k+1}$, where $L$ is multiplication by the Kähler form. | The "original" holes of a shape, those that aren't just copies generated by the shape's complex structure. |
| **Principal SO-bundle** | A fiber bundle whose fibers are copies of the special orthogonal group $SO(n)$, acting freely. | A space where every point comes with the full set of ways of setting up right-handed, perpendicular axes there. |
| **Projective space** | The space of 1D lines through the origin in a vector space $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ or $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$. | The space of directions. In it parallel lines finally meet, as at the horizon in a perspective drawing. |
| **Proper and smooth morphisms** | A map where preimages of compact sets are compact (proper) and the differential is surjective (smooth). | A well-behaved family of shapes: it doesn't rip holes, lose edges or create sharp corners in any member. |
| **Pseudo-isotopy** | A diffeomorphism of $M \times [0,1]$ restricting to the identity on $M \times \{0\}$. | Morphing a shape over time where the start is untouched, but the end might be twisted. |
| **Ramified covering** | A map that is a covering space everywhere except at a few branch points. | A spiralling parking garage where most floors stack normally, but all pinch together into a single point at the central shaft. |
| **Reciprocal image** | The pullback of a sheaf or bundle along a morphism. | Carrying a function or field from a destination shape backwards onto the starting shape. |
| **Reeb component** | A solid torus foliated by planes that spiral asymptotically into the boundary torus. | A layered shape where infinitely many spiralling layers are trapped inside a single donut skin. |
| **Regular functions** | Functions on an algebraic variety locally expressed as the quotient of polynomials with non-vanishing denominator. | Algebraic functions that don't shoot off to infinity or do anything unpredictable. |
| **Relative second cohomology lattice** | The family of second cohomology groups $H^2(X_s, \mathbb{Z})$ varying over a base space. | A grid tracking the 2D holes of a shape as the shape itself is slowly deformed. |
| **Ricci tensor** | A trace of the Riemann curvature tensor, measuring the volume deviation of geodesic balls. | A measurement of how much a curved space compresses or expands volume compared to flat space, direction by direction. |
| **Riemannian manifold** | A smooth manifold equipped with a positive-definite inner product on the tangent space. | Any curved space (like the Earth) with a consistent rule for measuring distance and angles. |
| **Riemannian metric** | The smooth symmetric tensor field defining lengths and angles ($g_{ij}$). | The actual ruler-formula used to calculate how far apart things are on a curved hill. |
| **Ring of quotients** | The localization of a ring obtained by inverting a set of non-zero-divisors. | Taking a rigid number system (like the integers) and forcing it to allow division (creating fractions). |
| **Root system** | A highly symmetric configuration of vectors in a Euclidean space governing Lie algebra structure. | A balanced crystal of arrows that dictates every allowed symmetry in a system. |
| **Saddle singularities** | A critical point of index 1 (or $n-1$), where the space curves up in some directions and down in others. For a foliation of a surface ("selle à quatre branches"): a point where four half-leaves meet. | The exact centre of a Pringles crisp: sloping up one way and down the other. |
| **Self-intersection** | The intersection number of a submanifold with a slight perturbation of itself. | How many times a curve on a shape meets a slightly nudged copy of itself, counted with signs. |
| **Serre duality** | An isomorphism $H^i(X, E) \cong H^{n-i}(X, E^* \otimes K_X)^*$ on a compact complex manifold. | A mirror: calculating the complex holes at the "top" of a shape is the same as calculating them at the "bottom". |
| **Signature** | The number of positive minus the number of negative eigenvalues of a symmetric bilinear form; for a manifold, of the form that intersects its middle-dimensional cycles. | A tally of how many independent directions count "plus" against how many count "minus". |
| **Simply connected algebraic surfaces** | A complex 2D variety (real 4D) where every loop can be shrunk to a point ($\pi_1 = 0$). | A 4D algebraic shape with no tunnel you could throw a lasso through. |
| **Special Kummer surface** | A K-3 surface obtained from an Abelian surface by folding it by $x \mapsto -x$ and repairing the 16 fixed points (blowing them up). | A highly symmetrical 4D shape built by folding a 4D donut in half and smoothing its 16 sharp corners. |
| **Spectral decomposition** | Breaking a space into invariant subspaces corresponding to eigenvalues of an operator. In dynamics (Smale): breaking the non-wandering set into finitely many closed invariant pieces, the basic sets. | Splitting white light through a prism into pure colours; in dynamics, splitting the recurring part of a system into its separate self-contained whirlpools. |
| **Spin manifold** | An oriented manifold whose second Stiefel-Whitney class $w_2$ is zero, allowing spinors to be globally defined. | A space that is "untangled" enough to let quantum spinning particles travel all the way round it without contradiction. |
| **Spin representation** | A fundamental representation of the Spin group that doesn't factor through SO(n). | The maths behind the rule that an electron must be turned through 720 degrees, not 360, to come back to itself. |
| **Spin structures** | A choice of principal $Spin(n)$ bundle lifting the tangent frame bundle. The choices are counted by $H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}_2)$. | Choosing how a quantum particle's sign behaves as it is carried round each loop of the shape: one two-way choice for each independent loop. |
| **Spinor laplacian** | The square of the Dirac operator ($D^2$), analogous to the standard Laplacian but for spinors. | The operator that calculates the energy and diffusion of quantum spin fields, smoothing them out. |
| **Stable/unstable foliations** | The foliations whose leaves are the stable (unstable) manifolds; they are tangent to the contracting (expanding) sub-bundles. | The invisible tracks in a moving fluid along which everything is either being squeezed together or pulled apart. |
| **Stable/unstable manifold** | For a point $x$: the set of points whose future (past) positions approach those of $x$. | The sheet of all points that end up travelling together with a given point (stable), or that were together with it in the distant past (unstable). |
| **Stalk** | The direct limit of sections of a sheaf over all open sets containing a specific point. | The infinitely zoomed-in essence of a family of functions at exactly one point. |
| **Tangent bundle** | The vector bundle formed by the disjoint union of all tangent spaces of a manifold. | The collection of all the flat planes touching a curved surface, one at every point. |
| **Tensor algebra** | The most general algebra containing a vector space, formed by all tensor powers $V^{\otimes k}$. | The sandbox where you can multiply vectors together in every combination without any restrictions. |
| **Todd genus** | The number obtained by evaluating the Todd class (a combination of Chern classes) on a complex manifold; by Riemann–Roch it equals the alternating count of holomorphic forms. | A topological recipe that counts, net, how many independent complex-smooth functions and forms a shape can support. |
| **Topological invariants** | Properties of a space that remain unchanged under homeomorphisms. | The deep structural fingerprints of a shape that survive however much you stretch or squash it. |
| **Topologically conjugate** | Two maps $f$, $g$ linked by a homeomorphism $h$ with $hf = gh$. | Two systems that may look completely different but follow the same choreography once one is redrawn on rubber. The redrawing may crease, so it need not respect sizes or smoothness. |
| **Toral automorphism** | The map of the torus $T^n$ given by an integer matrix of determinant $\pm 1$. (Newhouse, p. 761, also requires no eigenvalue on the unit circle.) | Taking a donut, slicing it open, stretching and shearing it, and wrapping it back onto itself so that it fits exactly. |
| **Torelli problem** | The question of whether a variety is uniquely determined up to isomorphism by its Hodge structure. | Asking if you can rebuild a shape exactly just by looking at a summary list of its holes. |
| **Transcendental cycles** | Homology classes on a complex algebraic variety that are perpendicular to all the classes of algebraic subvarieties. | Ghosts on a shape: holes that exist geometrically but cannot be described using algebra. |
| **Transversely affine** | A foliation equipped with local charts mapping to $\mathbb{R}$ with affine transition functions. | A sliced-up shape where moving from one layer to the next follows strict straight-line rules (shift and rescale). |
| **Transversely projective** | A foliation with transition functions that are projective transformations (Möbius transformations). | A sliced-up shape where the layers warp and zoom into each other as in a perspective drawing. |
| **Triple complexes** | A 3D array of modules with three differentials, each of which applied twice gives zero, anti-commuting in pairs. | A 3D spreadsheet cube of algebra where differentiating twice in any one of the three directions gives zero. |
| **Unimodular lattice** | A free abelian group with an integer-valued symmetric bilinear form of determinant $\pm 1$. | A perfectly packed, infinite grid of dots where the basic building block has volume exactly 1. |
| **Vector bundle** | A topological space formed by attaching a vector space continuously to every point of a base manifold. | Pinning a flat coordinate grid to every single point of a bumpy surface. |
| **W-limit set (ω-limit set)** | The set of all accumulation points of the forward orbit of a point in a dynamical system. | The ultimate destination: where a moving particle ends up hovering for ever. |
| **Weyl chamber** | The connected components of the complement of the hyperplanes orthogonal to the roots in a Lie algebra. | The fundamental "slice of pie" that contains all the non-redundant symmetry information of a system. |
| **Weyl group** | The finite group generated by reflections orthogonal to the roots of a Lie algebra. | The kaleidoscope that reflects that "slice of pie" to generate the entire symmetrical structure. |

## Part B: terms used in M1 and in the discussion of J2

| The Pure Math Jargon | Your Freshman Translation | The Plain English Intuition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Almost every (typical)** | A statement holds for almost every point if the points where it fails form a set of measure zero. | True for every point you would ever hit by chance; the exceptions exist but take up no room. |
| **Almost-quaternionic structure** | Three almost complex structures $I, J, K$ on the tangent spaces, given locally, with $IJ = K = -JI$. | A copy of the quaternion multiplication table acting on the directions at every point. |
| **Anti-involution** | A map $a \mapsto a^*$ of an algebra with $(ab)^* = b^*a^*$ and $a^{**} = a$. | A flip that also reverses the order in which things are done. |
| **Anticanonical line ($K^{-1}$)** | The top exterior power of the holomorphic tangent bundle of a complex manifold; the dual of the canonical bundle. On $\mathbb{CP}^2$ it is $\mathcal{O}(3)$. | The line attached at every point that measures complex volume the 'upright' way round. On the projective plane it winds three times. |
| **Branch point** | A point of the base of a ramified covering over which the sheets come together. | The central shaft of the parking garage: the one place where the two floors are the same point. |
| **Clifford action** | The rule by which each tangent vector $v$ acts on spinors as a linear map, with $v\cdot v = -\lvert v\rvert^2$. | The standard machine that lets an arrow at a point act on a spinor at that point. |
| **Closed surface** | A compact two-dimensional manifold without boundary. | A skin with no edge and no puncture: a ball's skin, a donut's skin, a pretzel's skin. |
| **Commute** | Two maps $f$, $g$ commute if $fg = gf$. | Two moves that give the same result whichever is done first. |
| **Complex line (in $\mathbb{C}^3$)** | A one-dimensional complex subspace: all complex multiples $\lambda v$ of one non-zero vector $v$. | A direction for a complex-valued arrow: the arrow with its size and its phase both forgotten. |
| **Complex projective plane ($\mathbb{CP}^2$)** | The set of complex lines through the origin in $\mathbb{C}^3$; a compact space of four real dimensions. | The space of all directions that three complex numbers taken together can point in. |
| **Complex-linear map** | A map $A$ with $A(v+w)=Av+Aw$ and $A(\lambda v)=\lambda Av$ for every complex number $\lambda$. | A move that respects adding arrows and respects multiplying them by complex numbers. |
| **Complex-valued (complexified) units, $\mathbb{C}\otimes\operatorname{Im}\mathbb{H}$** | Combinations $z_1 i+z_2 j+z_3 k$ with complex coefficients $z_1,z_2,z_3$; as a space, $\mathbb{C}^3$. | The three imaginary units with complex numbers, and not only ordinary numbers, allowed as the amounts of each. |
| **Cone point** | A point of an orbifold whose neighbourhood is a disc folded by a rotation of order $n$; the angle round it is $2\pi/n$. | The tip of a paper cone. |
| **Conjugate (matrices, group elements)** | $B = SAS^{-1}$ for an invertible $S$ of the allowed kind. | The same move, described in relabelled coordinates. |
| **Countable** | A set whose elements can be listed $1, 2, 3, \dots$ | Infinitely many, perhaps, but so sparse that a dart thrown at random never hits one. |
| **Covering (deck) involution** | The map of a double cover that exchanges the two points over each point of the base. | Swapping the two floors of the parking garage. |
| **Determinant** | For a $2\times2$ matrix, $ad - bc$: the factor by which the matrix scales area, with a minus sign if it flips handedness. | +1: keeps area and handedness. −1: keeps area and turns a left hand into a right hand. |
| **Determinant line bundle (of a Spin^c structure)** | The complex line bundle $L$ that a $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure carries; its first Chern class is $w_2$ modulo 2. | The extra "phase line" that makes spinors possible on a shape that would otherwise not allow them. |
| **Division algebra** | An algebra in which every non-zero element has an inverse. Over the reals, of finite dimension and associative: $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$ or $\mathbb{H}$ (Frobenius). | A number system in which everything except zero can be undone. |
| **Divisor (on a curve)** | A finite list of points, each with a whole-number weight. | A shopping list of where a function is allowed zeros and poles, and how many. |
| **Eigenvalue** | A number $\lambda$ with $Av = \lambda v$ for some non-zero $v$. | A direction that the matrix does not turn, and the factor by which it stretches that direction. |
| **Euler characteristic** | Vertices − edges + faces (and so on) of any triangulation; for a closed orientable surface, $2 - 2g$. | A single whole number that does not change however the shape is cut into pieces: 2 for a ball, 0 for a donut. |
| **Factor (of a product)** | One of the spaces $A$, $B$ in a product $A\times B$, a point of which is a point of each at once. | One of the building blocks multiplied together to make the shape. M1 also says 'piece'. |
| **Fibre (of a bundle)** | In a space built as a family of copies of one shape, one copy for each point of a base, the copy sitting over one point. | One donut skin out of the ring of donut skins: the one belonging to a single moment. |
| **Gradient ($\nabla w$)** | The vector of rates of change of a function $w$ in each direction; it is zero where $w$ is level, for instance at a highest or lowest point. | The arrow pointing straight uphill, as long as the hill is steep. On a summit there is no arrow. |
| **Hypothesis (of a statement)** | The "if" part of a statement: what is supposed, and not proved, in order to conclude the rest. | What you are asked to grant before the conclusion is drawn. |
| **Fixed point** | A point $x$ with $f(x) = x$. | A point that the move leaves where it is. |
| **Flow** | A family of maps $\varphi_t$, one for each time $t$, with $\varphi_{s+t} = \varphi_s \varphi_t$. | A rule that carries every point along as time passes. |
| **First homology of the torus (the action on loops)** | The group $H_1(T^2) = \mathbb{Z}^2$: a loop on the torus is recorded by two whole numbers, how often it winds each way round. A map of the torus to itself moves these pairs by a $2\times2$ whole-number matrix. | Every loop on a donut skin has two winding counts. Any redrawing of the skin changes the counts by a fixed table of four whole numbers, however much it wobbles the skin locally. |
| **Genus** | For a closed orientable surface, the number of handles $g$. | How many holes the donut has: 0 for a ball, 1 for a donut, 3 for a three-holed pretzel. |
| **GL₂(ℤ) and SL₂(ℤ)** | $2\times2$ integer matrices with determinant $\pm1$ (GL), or exactly $+1$ (SL). | The ways of relabelling a square grid onto itself. SL keeps handedness; GL may flip it. |
| **Homeomorphism** | A continuous bijection with a continuous inverse. | Morphing a rubber sheet without tearing or gluing. Creases and kinks are allowed (in a *diffeomorphism* they are not). |
| **Fibonacci matrix** | The matrix with rows $(1,1)$, $(1,0)$; it sends a pair of consecutive Fibonacci numbers to the next pair. Determinant $-1$; eigenvalues $\phi$ and $-1/\phi$. | One move of the Fibonacci rule "add the last two". It stretches by the golden ratio and flips handedness. |
| **Hyperbolic matrix** | An integer matrix with no eigenvalue of modulus one. In $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$: trace of size greater than 2. | A matrix that genuinely stretches one direction and squeezes another, with no direction merely turned or left alone. |
| **Homotopic maps** | Two maps $f$, $g$ joined by a continuous family of maps $f_s$ with $f_0 = f$, $f_1 = g$. Homotopic maps of the torus have the same matrix on loops. | Two redrawings that can be slid into one another without tearing: the same in the large, different in the detail. |
| **Involution** | A map $\sigma$ with $\sigma\sigma = $ identity. | A flip: do it twice and you are back where you started. |
| **Lebesgue measure** | The ordinary notion of length, area or volume. | Size as a ruler would measure it. |
| **Lift (of a map through a covering)** | A map $\tilde f$ upstairs with $p\tilde f = f p$, where $p$ is the covering. | Doing the same move on the parking garage that was done on the ground plan beneath it. |
| **Line bundle; its degree** | A complex line attached to each point. On a surface its degree (first Chern class) is the number of zeros minus the number of poles of any of its sections. | A twisting line over the shape, and a whole number saying how many times it twists. |
| **Mapping torus (suspension)** | From a map $f$ of $X$: the space $X \times [0,1]$ with $(x,1)$ glued to $(f(x),0)$. It carries a flow whose return map is $f$. | Turn a move into a movie: go once round a circle of time, and what comes back is the shape after one move. |
| **Measure** | A rule giving each (measurable) set a size $\ge 0$, the size of a union of separate pieces being the sum of their sizes. Length, area, volume and probability are measures. | A way of saying how much room a set of points takes up. Different rulers can give different answers, so one must say which ruler. |
| **Measure zero** | A set that can be covered by pieces of total size as small as you like. | Negligible: there, but taking up no room. |
| **Norm form ($N(a) = aa^*$)** | The number got by multiplying an element by its flipped partner; for a quaternion, the sum of the squares of its four coordinates. It is positive-definite when it is positive for every non-zero element. | The squared size of an element. If every non-zero element has a positive size, every one can be undone. |
| **Orbifold** | A space that is locally a manifold divided by a finite group. | A smooth shape with a few sharp points or folds. |
| **Orbit (flow line)** | For a flow $\varphi_t$ and a point $x$: the curve $t \mapsto \varphi_t(x)$. | The path one point traces as time passes. |
| **Open arc** | A circle with one point removed; it can be laid out as an interval of the line without its end points, so its points have an order. | A loop of string cut at one place and pulled straight: now it has a left and a right. |
| **Orientation (handedness)** | A consistent choice, over a whole surface or space, of which way round is "anticlockwise". A map preserves it if it carries anticlockwise to anticlockwise; a linear map does so exactly when its determinant is positive. | The difference between a left glove and a right glove. A map that keeps handedness never turns the glove inside out. |
| **Periodic point** | A point $x$ with $f^n(x) = x$ for some $n \ge 1$. | A point that comes back to exactly where it started after some number of moves. |
| **Phase (of a complex number)** | For $z\neq0$, the angle $\theta$ in $z=\lvert z\rvert e^{i\theta}$, defined up to whole turns. | Where on the clock face a complex number points, as distinct from how big it is. |
| **Pillowcase** | The torus divided by $z \mapsto -z$: a sphere with four cone points of angle $\pi$. | A donut folded in half and sewn: a pillow with four corners. |
| **Pseudo-Anosov map** | A homeomorphism of a surface with two crossing families of leaves, one stretched by $\lambda$ and the other shrunk by $1/\lambda$, except at finitely many points where three or more leaves meet. | A taffy-puller that works everywhere except at a few pinch points. |
| **Pull-back ($\iota^{*}$)** | For a map $\iota$ and a function $w$: the function $\iota^{*}w$ whose value at $x$ is $w(\iota(x))$. The pull-back of a constant is the same constant. | Reading a quantity off at the place the flip sends you to, instead of where you stand. |
| **Quarter-turn ($S$)** | The matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ acting on the torus: rotation by a right angle. For M1's step, $SM_0S^{-1} = M_0^{-1}$. | Turning the donut skin through a right angle, so that its two directions change places. |
| **Quaternions ($\mathbb{H}$)** | The four-dimensional number system with units $1, i, j, k$ and $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$. | Numbers for which the order of multiplying matters: $ij = k$ but $ji = -k$. |
| **Quotient $G/H$** | The set of cosets $gH$ of a subgroup $H$ in a group $G$; when $G$ acts transitively on a set and $H$ is the subgroup of all elements that fix one point, the set itself. | All the moves, with two moves counted as one when they send the chosen thing to the same place: so, the list of places it can be sent. |
| **Rational point (of the torus)** | A point of $\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ both of whose coordinates are fractions $p/q$. | A point of the donut skin whose two angles are both exact fractions of a full turn. |
| **Return map (Poincaré map)** | For a flow that keeps crossing a slice: the map sending a point of the slice to where it next crosses. | Photograph the flow once per lap; the return map takes each photograph to the next. |
| **Riemannian volume (area)** | The measure that a Riemannian metric defines: the size of a region computed from the lengths the metric gives. On a surface it is the area. | The amount of room a region takes up when measured with the shape's own ruler. |
| **Riemann–Hurwitz count** | For a cover of degree $d$ of surfaces: $\chi(\text{cover}) = d\,\chi(\text{base}) -$ (number of sheets lost at the branch points). | The bookkeeping that tells how many holes the parking garage has, from the ground plan and the number of shafts. |
| **Smooth** | Differentiable as many times as you like. | No kinks or creases anywhere, however closely you look. |
| **Sol geometry** | $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the metric $ds^2 + e^{2s}dx^2 + e^{-2s}dy^2$ (one of Thurston's eight geometries), and the closed shapes cut from it, such as the torus bundle of a hyperbolic matrix. | A stack of sheets where, as you climb, every sheet is drawn wider one way and narrower the other, by compound interest, its area never changing. |
| **Special unitary group ($SU(3)$)** | The elements of $U(3)$ with determinant one. | The length-keeping turnings of three complex directions with the overall phase taken out. |
| **Spectral radius** | The largest modulus of an eigenvalue. | The strongest stretch the matrix performs. |
| **Spin^c structure** | A lift of the frame bundle to the group $\mathrm{Spin}^c(n) = \mathrm{Spin}(n) \times_{\pm1} U(1)$; it exists when $w_2$ is the reduction of an integer class. | Spinors made possible by pairing them with a phase line, on a shape that does not allow spinors by themselves. |
| **Spinor** | An element of a space on which the Clifford algebra, and so the Spin group, acts. | The kind of object that must be turned twice round to return to itself. |
| **Split quaternions** | The four-dimensional algebra with units $1, i, j, k$ in which $i^2 = -1$ but $j^2 = k^2 = +1$; the same thing as the $2\times2$ real matrices $M_2(\mathbb{R})$. | The quaternion table with two of its three squares changed from $-1$ to $+1$. Some non-zero elements then cannot be undone. |
| **Stabiliser** | The subgroup of elements of a group action that fix a given point or structure (the isotropy subgroup of Part A). | The moves that leave the chosen thing exactly where it is. |
| **Stiefel–Whitney class $w_2$** | A class in $H^2(M;\mathbb{Z}_2)$ of an oriented manifold; it vanishes exactly when the manifold is spin. | The yes/no test for whether spinors can live on a shape. |
| **Subalgebra** | A subset of an algebra that contains $1$ and is closed under adding, scaling and multiplying. | A smaller number system living inside the big one. |
| **Symmetry group** | The set of all maps of an object to itself that keep a stated structure, with composition as the product. | All the ways of moving a thing that leave it looking the same in the respect one cares about. |
| **Topological entropy** | The exponential rate at which the number of orbit segments that can be told apart grows with their length. For a hyperbolic integer matrix on the torus: the logarithm of the spectral radius. | How fast the system manufactures unpredictability. Zero means the far future can be read off from the present. |
| **Torus ($T^2$)** | The product of two circles, $S^1 \times S^1$; equally, the plane with points identified when they differ by whole numbers, $\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$. A surface: two dimensions. | The skin of a donut. A point on it is given by two angles. |
| **Torus bundle over the circle** | A three-dimensional space built from $T^2 \times [0,1]$ by gluing the torus at the end to the torus at the start by a map $f$ of the torus: the mapping torus of $f$. When $f$ is a hyperbolic matrix it is a "Sol" torus bundle. | A ring of donut skins, one for each moment round a circle; going once round, the skin comes back redrawn by the map. |
| **Trace** | For a $2\times2$ matrix, $a + d$: the sum of the eigenvalues. | A single number that, with the determinant, tells how strongly the matrix stretches. |
| **Transpose** | The matrix got by exchanging rows and columns; $(AB)^T = B^T A^T$. | Flipping a grid of numbers across its diagonal; a flip that reverses the order of products. |
| **Unit group (of $\mathbb{R} \oplus \mathbb{R}u$)** | The elements $x + yu$ of size one, $x^2 - u^2y^2 = 1$: a circle when $u^2 = -1$, a hyperbola when $u^2 = +1$. It is compact when it is closed and bounded. | The size-one elements of a little number system. Either they go round and come back (a circle), or they run off for ever (a hyperbola). |
| **Unit quaternions ($\mathrm{Sp}(1)$)** | The quaternions of length one; as a shape, the three-sphere $S^3$. | The rotations of three-dimensional space, each counted twice. |
| **Unitary group ($U(3)$, $U(2)$, $U(1)$)** | The complex-linear maps of $\mathbb{C}^n$ that keep the length $(\sum\lvert z_a\rvert^2)^{1/2}$; $U(1)$ is the circle of phases. | The turnings of $n$ complex directions among themselves that change no lengths. |
| **Warped product** | $B \times F$ with the metric $g_B + w^2 g_F$, $w$ a positive function on $B$. | Two shapes multiplied together, where the size of the second depends on where you stand in the first, as circles of latitude shrink towards the poles. |
| **Warping function ($w$)** | The positive function $w$ in a warped product: the lengths of the second shape are multiplied by $w$, and $w$ varies over the first. | The zoom setting: a number, changing from place to place, that says how big the other shape is drawn there. |
| **Frame (of the tangent spaces)** | At each point, an ordered basis of the tangent space; a global frame is a choice of basis at every point that varies smoothly and never degenerates. | A set of axes planted at every point of the shape, all of them fitting together with no snags anywhere. |
| **Parallelisable** | A manifold admitting a global frame; equivalently its tangent bundle is trivial, $TM \cong M \times \mathbb{R}^n$. | A shape on which you can lay down the same axes everywhere at once. The two-sphere is not: you cannot comb a hairy ball flat. |
| **Vector field** | A smooth choice of tangent vector at each point of a manifold. | An arrow drawn at every point of the shape, the arrows varying smoothly. |
| **Left translation** | On a Lie group $G$, the map $L_g(x) = gx$; it carries the tangent space at the identity to the tangent space at $g$. | Sliding the whole group along by one of its own elements, which copies the axes at the starting point to every other point. |
| **Self-dual two-forms ($\Lambda^2_+$)** | On an oriented four-manifold with a metric, the rank-three sub-bundle of two-forms fixed by the Hodge star ($*\alpha = \alpha$). | Three directions' worth of "area measurements" that a four-dimensional shape carries of its own accord. On four dimensions they are what supplies the $I, J, K$. |
| **Rank (of a vector bundle)** | The dimension of the vector space attached to each point. | How many independent directions the backpack at each point holds. |
| **Reduction of the structure group** | A choice, consistent over the whole manifold, of the smaller group $H \subset G$ in which the transition maps of a bundle may be taken to lie. | Narrowing the kinds of turning you allow between neighbouring patches, and asking whether the narrowing can be made everywhere at once rather than patch by patch. |
| **Obstruction** | A characteristic class whose non-vanishing proves that a reduction, a section or a lift cannot exist globally. | A number or barcode computed from the shape that says "this cannot be done", without trying to do it. |
| **$\mathrm{Sp}(3)\mathrm{Sp}(1)$** | The subgroup of $SO(12)$ given by $\mathbb{H}^3$ with quaternion scalars on one side and quaternionic-linear maps on the other, the two $\pm1$'s identified. | The group that says a twelve-dimensional shape is made of three quaternion directions, with the copy of the quaternions itself allowed to turn from place to place. |
| **Homotopy type** | The equivalence class of a space under maps that can be continuously deformed into one another; it is what homology, $\chi$ and the characteristic classes see. | What a shape is if you may stretch and bend it but not cut or glue. Changing only the metric, as a warp does, leaves it alone. |
| **$\mathrm{Sp}(1)\mathrm{Sp}(1)=SO(4)$** | The two-to-one map $\mathrm{Sp}(1)\times\mathrm{Sp}(1)\to SO(4)$, $(p,q)\cdot x = px\bar q$, with kernel $\pm(1,1)$. | Every rotation of four-dimensional space is a quaternion multiplying on the left and another on the right. This is why four dimensions carry the quaternion table for free. |
| **Relabelling (of a shape)** | A one-to-one map of the shape onto itself, or onto another shape, smooth in both directions (a diffeomorphism; in M1's definition of "the same geometry", smooth away from the cone points), used to carry every point, and whatever is defined at the points, across. In M1 the word is the paper's own (canon v6.3 ll.249, 264, 271, 297). | Renaming the points without tearing or gluing. If renaming the points of one shape turns it exactly into another, the two were one shape written down in two ways. |
| **Mirror image (of a shape or a piece)** | The same shape with its handedness reversed: what an orientation-reversing relabelling produces. For the torus $\R^{2}/\Z^{2}$, a whole-number matrix of determinant $-1$. | A left glove and a right glove. Point for point they match, but no turning of one in its own space makes it the other. |
| **Same up to (something)** | Two objects are "the same up to X" when an operation of kind X carries one to the other; differences that X removes are not counted as differences. | Two maps of one town drawn at different scales are the same up to scale. Saying what X is, is saying what counts as a real difference. |
| **Rigour tag ([R], [D], [M])** | A label on a statement saying how firmly it is established. In M1: [R] proved, or a cited classical theorem; [D] derived under a stated reading; [M] motivated. | A hallmark on a piece of silver: it does not change the piece, it tells you how it was tested. A row of a table cannot carry a higher mark than the weakest result it leans on. |
| **Zero and pole (of a section or a vector field); their order** | A zero is a point where the object vanishes, a pole a point where it blows up; the order says how fast (like $w^{2}$ at $w=0$: order two). On a closed surface, zeros less poles of any section of a line bundle is the bundle's degree. | Count the calm spots in the wind on a globe, each with its weight, and subtract the gusts that run off to infinity: the answer is the same whatever wind you draw, and for the sphere's own tangent directions it is two. |
