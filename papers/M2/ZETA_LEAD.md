# ζ — the lead "an accumulated angle along the fibres" (10 November 2026)

**The lead (PI):** "zeta couples the pillowcase (T²/ℤ₂) to S³ × S¹ Einstein space time through the fibres." The blind check of `papers/M1/FACTOR_OF_TWO.md` set the condition: a total such as 12π must be the value of a named *additive* quantity, named before anything is counted.

**Protocol** (`checks/turning_check.py`, header): two additive quantities were named first, then computed; both results are reported as they came out. **Not blind-checked yet.**

## Q1. The turning accumulated along the fibres that close in one circuit

The fibres of the drive's flow that close after one circuit of time are the three returned points. The turning along each, per circuit, is the rotation part of the return map there.

| Fibre | Return map | Turning per circuit |
|---|---|---|
| through the corner P₁ | M₀ (both eigenvalues positive) | 0 |
| through each of the two ordinary returned points | −M₀ = a half-turn times M₀ | π each |
| **sum** | | **2π** |

**The lead, taken literally, gives 2π per circuit, not 12π.** (It is the same 2 as the fixed-point sum the blind check named.)

## Q2. Turning measured by the torus's own additive count

The symmetries of the torus are generated by two elementary shears, a and b, with one relation, a b a = b a b. Writing an element as a product of shears and adding the exponents is additive by construction. CHECKED:

| Element | As shears | Count |
|---|---|---|
| the quarter-turn S (M2's exchange of growth and decay is S with a reversal of the flow) | a b a | **3** |
| the fold −I (the involution of A2 as M1 has it; S done twice) | (a b)³ | **6** |
| a full turn (the fold done twice; closure) | (a b)⁶ | **12** |
| the drive M₀ | a b⁻¹ | **0** net |

One shear is a twelfth of a full turn. The count is defined only up to twelve, because twelve shears in this order make the identity.

**What this shows.** There is an additive quantity, intrinsic to the pillowcase's torus and owing nothing to ℂP² or S², in which **closure is twelve**: a full turn is twelve elementary shears. And the PI's factors appear in it as successive doublings: the exchange is 3; done twice it is the fold, 6; done twice again it is a full turn, 12. That is "3 × 2 × 2" with each factor a definite operation: the quarter-turn, then squaring to the fold (A2), then squaring to a full turn (A1). The spinor's sign would double it once more, to 24, the number found in M1's algebra (`papers/M1/FACTOR_OF_TWO.md` §2).

**What it does not show.**

- **It gives 12, not 12π.** In this count a full turn is 12 units and is an angle of 2π, so one unit is π/6. For ζ = 12π one would need each shear to contribute π, where its share of a full turn is π/6. The PI's sentence, "the fibres land on the pillowcase each contributing π, there are 12 of them", has this shape; "π each" is not derived.
- **The drive itself counts zero.** It is one shear forward and one back. So the twelve is not "what the drive accumulates"; it is what closure costs.
- **The 3 here is the length of a quarter-turn, not obviously the three generations.** Whether it is the same three as the trace of the drive or the three returned points is not shown.
- **"Twelve is cheap."** This is one more route to twelve. What distinguishes it from the others is only that it is additive and belongs to the pillowcase alone; that makes it a candidate for the quantity ζ measures, not a derivation.

M1 Proposition 8.2 takes the flux of the twist's line through S² to be Q_w = 6 units ("three units of χ(S²) = 2", l.550), and a flux of 6 units is a total phase of 2π × 6 = 12π.
