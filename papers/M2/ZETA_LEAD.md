# ζ — the lead "an accumulated angle along the fibres" (10 November 2026)

**The lead (PI):** "zeta couples the pillowcase (T²/ℤ₂) to S³ × S¹ Einstein space time through the fibres." The blind check of `papers/M1/FACTOR_OF_TWO.md` set the condition: a total such as 12π must be the value of a named *additive* quantity, named before anything is counted.

**Protocol** (`checks/turning_check.py`, header): two additive quantities were named first, then computed; both results are reported as they came out. **Not blind-checked yet.**

## Q1. The turning accumulated along the fibres that close in one circuit

The fibres of the drive's flow that close after one circuit of time are the three returned points. The turning along each, per circuit, is the rotation part of the return map there.

| Fibre | Return map | Turning per circuit |
|---|---|---|
| through the corner P₁ | M₀ (both eigenvalues positive) | 0 |
| through each of the two ordinary returned points | −M₀ = a half-turn times M₀ | π each |
| **sum** | | **2π** |

**The lead, taken literally, gives 2π per circuit, not 12π.** (It is the same 2 as the fixed-point sum the blind check named.)

## Q2. Turning measured by the torus's own additive count

The symmetries of the torus are generated by two elementary shears, a and b, with one relation, a b a = b a b. Writing an element as a product of shears and adding the exponents is additive by construction. CHECKED:

| Element | As shears | Count |
|---|---|---|
| the quarter-turn S (M2's exchange of growth and decay is S with a reversal of the flow) | a b a | **3** |
| the fold −I (the involution of A2 as M1 has it; S done twice) | (a b)³ | **6** |
| a full turn (the fold done twice; closure) | (a b)⁶ | **12** |
| the drive M₀ | a b⁻¹ | **0** net |

One shear is a twelfth of a full turn. The count is defined only up to twelve, because twelve shears in this order make the identity.

**What this shows.** There is an additive quantity, intrinsic to the pillowcase's torus and owing nothing to ℂP² or S², in which **closure is twelve**: a full turn is twelve elementary shears. And the PI's factors appear in it as successive doublings: the exchange is 3; done twice it is the fold, 6; done twice again it is a full turn, 12. That is "3 × 2 × 2" with each factor a definite operation: the quarter-turn, then squaring to the fold (A2), then squaring to a full turn (A1). The spinor's sign would double it once more, to 24, the number found in M1's algebra (`papers/M1/FACTOR_OF_TWO.md` §2).

**What it does not show.**

- **It gives 12, not 12π.** In this count a full turn is 12 units and is an angle of 2π, so one unit is π/6. For ζ = 12π one would need each shear to contribute π, where its share of a full turn is π/6. The PI's sentence, "the fibres land on the pillowcase each contributing π, there are 12 of them", has this shape; "π each" is not derived.
- **The drive itself counts zero.** It is one shear forward and one back. So the twelve is not "what the drive accumulates"; it is what closure costs.
- **The 3 here is the length of a quarter-turn, not obviously the three generations.** Whether it is the same three as the trace of the drive or the three returned points is not shown.
- **"Twelve is cheap."** This is one more route to twelve. What distinguishes it from the others is only that it is additive and belongs to the pillowcase alone; that makes it a candidate for the quantity ζ measures, not a derivation.

## What on the pillowcase is worth π? (continued, 10 November 2026)

**Named quantity:** the angle of turning, which is additive, measured two ways that must agree: as the angle missing at a cone point (M1 Thm 5.7), and as shears times π/6 (Q2 above: twelve shears are a full turn of 2π).

| Thing | Angle missing (M1) | In shears (Q2) | Shears × π/6 |
|---|---|---|---|
| one corner of the pillowcase (one fold) | π | 6 | π |
| the four corners together | 4π (Gauss–Bonnet, χ = 2) | 24 | 4π |
| one elementary shear | — | 1 | π/6 |
| a full turn | 2π | 12 | 2π |

The two measures agree line by line (arithmetic CHECKED: 6 × π/6 = π; 4 × 6 = 24; 24 × π/6 = 4π = 2π × 2).

**Answer: the thing worth π is a corner, that is, one fold. A shear is worth π/6.**

**Consequences.**

1. **"Twelve things each worth π" cannot be the twelve shears of a full turn.** Those are worth 2π in total.
2. **The pillowcase's own total is 4π**, four corners at π each. This is a theorem and it is additive.
3. **So ζ = 12π, if it is an angle of this kind, is three times the pillowcase's total: 12π = 3 × 4π**, which is the PI's "4 corners × 3 generations" with π for each corner. Equivalently it is twelve folds.
4. **The one factor not accounted for is the 3.** Everything else is M1: π per corner (Thm 5.7), four corners (Prop 5.3). In M1's own 12 = 3 · 2 · 2 the same 3 is χ(ℂP²) (Thm 7.6). On the PI's account it is the three generations: one thing, three shadows. On the counts of today it could be the trace of the drive, which is the number of points returned each circuit. Whether these are one three is not shown; and nothing yet says why the pillowcase's total should be taken three times.
5. **The 24 is the four corners in shears**, and it is the same 24 as the group generated by the corner units and the drive (`papers/M1/FACTOR_OF_TWO.md` §2), and twice M1's twelve.

**A place to look, checked against published sources on 10 November 2026** (`registers/Verified_References.md`, V-6a to V-6d). Confirmed from secondary sources: a singular fibre with monodromy −I (Kodaira type I₀*) has Euler number 6; the totals are 12 on a rational elliptic surface and 24 on a K3 surface; the pillowcase is the base of T⁴/ℤ₂ with four points of monodromy −1 (Sen); the Regge action is a sum over hinges of deficit angle times hinge volume. **Not confirmed:** that each elementary fibre is worth π/6 on the base (Greene, Shapere, Vafa and Yau: only the abstract was found), and the numerical factor in the Regge action. No primary source was read. So the recollection was right in its counts (6 per corner, 24 in all, and a known shape whose total is 12) and unverified in its angle. It remains a pointer and not a premise.

**Status.** The gap in ζ is now one item: *why three times.* That is the question the fifth axiom was said to answer (one thing, three shadows), so it is the PI's account that has to supply it, not a count.

## Why three times: the PI's guess, assessed (10 November 2026)

**The guess** (record §17): "the reason why it is x 3generations - is the same reason why M12 gives 3 - this is the reason, the shape itself is a third spin needs 3 spins to get back to unity".

**Disclosure.** The target 12π was known to me. The additive quantity was named first; the steps were then given to a blind checker without the target; its table was re-run here (`checks/third_spin_check.py`; `checks/turning_check.py`, last block).

**Named additive quantity.** R = the sum over the corner curves of (angle missing, π) × (length of the curve along time). This has the form of the Regge action (V-6d).

**What this says about the guess.**

1. **The first half is true of M1's shape.** "The shape itself is a third spin; needs three spins to get back to unity" is S2 and S5: on the corners the drive is a third of a turn, and three circuits bring every corner back. With π for each corner, four corners and three circuits, the named quantity is 12π. This is the first route today in which a total of 12π is the value of a named additive quantity, and it is the PI's "4 corners × 3" and "fibres landing on the pillowcase, each contributing π, twelve of them".
2. **The second half, "the same reason M₁₂ gives 3", is true only in part.** M1 has two threes here: the trace of the drive (three points returned; the minimal drive) and the three cycled corners (i, j, k; Prop 4.4). They are linked through parity alone: an odd trace gives a three-cycle. The three of "three spins" is the three-cycle, and it would be three for trace 5 or 7 as well. It is the three of M1's quaternion units (the third axiom meeting the fourth), and it is not the trace. Whether it is also χ(ℂP²) = 3 is not shown.
3. **The step that carries the whole weight is S7.** Per circuit the quantity is 4π, whatever the number of circuits. 12π is "4π times three circuits", so the derivation of ζ now rests on one sentence: the coupling is measured over the period in which the shape returns to unity, and not over one circuit. That sentence is the first axiom (closure) applied to the corners. Nothing in M1 or M2 states it, and without it the answer is 4π.
4. **Still open beyond S7:** why the coupling of Φ in the field equation is this quantity; the length of one circuit (R has the unit of a length, ζ is a number); and the fixed corner P₁, which is back after one circuit and is nonetheless counted three times.

**Failure test.** If the period over which the coupling is measured is one circuit, the quantity is 4π; if six, 24π. A derivation of S7 must exclude both.
