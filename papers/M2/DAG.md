## 1. What M2 is to derive (confirmed by the PI, Decision Log P181)

**The PI's words (18 November 2026):** "What is left for paper M2 to do is to derive the equation that naturally sits on the manifold.  It will need this result, to derive the equation.  How it is used to derive the equation is up to M2 using first principles."

**The read-back that the PI confirmed** ("I confirm the read-back of what M2 is supposed to derive"), as it was put. In it "I" and "mine" are the architect, and "your sentence" is the PI's. Everything in it other than the PI's quoted words is the architect's, confirmed by the PI.

- **Plain English:** M1 produced the shape. M2 starts from the six axioms and that shape with everything M1 fixed on it, and finds the equation that the shape and the axioms force, with nothing put in by hand. The result about the drive's element is available to M2. Where and how it enters is found in the derivation and not prescribed now.
- **Formal translation (mine):** a differential equation on the twelve-dimensional geometry of M1 Theorem 8.1. What it is an equation for, and its form, are outputs. "Naturally sits" means selected by the axioms and the three clauses of the sixth axiom, as M1's choices were. "First principles" means the six axioms and M1 v7.3 only.
- **What rests on it:** every card of the DAG; it is the end point they lead to.
- **Where others would hear something else:**
  - A physicist hears "the natural equation on a manifold" as a known one (Einstein's, Dirac's) or as whatever comes from a chosen action. Here nothing is chosen in advance.
  - A mathematician would ask "an equation for what unknown?", and would hear "natural" as a technical word about operators that respect symmetries.
  - A physicist hears "first principles" as symmetry and an action principle; here it means the axioms.
- **The opposite meaning the words allow:** "the equation" could mean one whose form is already known and is to be reached. I take it the other way: whatever the axioms force, with the comparison to any known form made only afterwards (the blind rule).
- **What it does not say:** whether M2 also has to settle the direction of the warp (the one item M1 leaves owed, in `papers/M2/OWED_BY_M2_FROM_M1.md`). M1 says that is fixed "with the field equation", so I take it as part of M2.
- **One point on M1's own text:** M1's outlook (l.1165) says more than your sentence: that the equation comes "with its coupling to the Coherent Field" and that "the two sheets of the involution of A2 and the non-commutative structure of A3 are the variables in which that equation is written". It is outlook and carries no proof. I would not treat it as a premise for M2 unless you say so.

**After the read-back (Decision Log P181).** Line 1165 of M1 v7.3 is the second paragraph of its Section 9. The PI confirmed the read-back and did not say that the sentence is a premise: **it is not a premise for M2.** "With the field equation" is from M1 v7.3, Section 1.4; "the direction of the warp" is the wording of `papers/M2/OWED_BY_M2_FROM_M1.md`.

**The one principle (the PI, 20 November 2026, Decision Log P262).** The wording that the PI confirmed ("1 confirmed as worded"), as it was read back; the wording is the architect's:

> "Naturally sits" (P181) is read as follows. The equation of M2 is, among the equations for a state: (i) made only from the operations that what is given determines with no further object; (ii) carried to itself by every map of the geometry that carries what is given to itself; (iii) selected by the three clauses of M1 Definition 3.2, as P181 has it: no free constant, nothing that is not needed, and the least. Which operations and which maps there are is computed from M1 and the entries, and is not declared. The candidates are listed, lowest order first, before anything is compared with them.

- **Plain English:** every equation that can be built from what the shape hands over is listed; those that the shape's own symmetries respect are kept; the least is taken. No further ruling is made on what an axiom means for the field unless the list leaves a choice.
- **What is new in it:** parts (i) and (ii). Part (iii) is the read-back of P181, at the head of this section ("selected by the axioms and the three clauses of the sixth axiom").
- **What rests on it:** the cards of Part III are redrawn around it; the open points of S09, S10 and S13 are decided by the list or come back to the PI as a choice; for S08 the thing to be closed is whatever the list brings.
- **One tension, not ruled:** the PI's musing (P216, a lead) has "because of asymmetry the order matters"; part (ii) keeps only the equations that every map of its kind respects. Whether time run backwards together with the quarter-turn, the map of M1, Proposition 5.8, that swaps $\kappa$ for $-\kappa$, is such a map is computed: of $\Mtwelve$ run backwards with the quarter-turn M1 has "that it carries every datum of $\Mtwelve$ to itself is not shown here" (Remark 3.4, l.291). That the map of Proposition 5.8 is the symmetry of M1, Definition 2.4(iii), for this geometry is the architect's reading of Proposition 5.8.
- **What would show it false:** no equation survives; or the survivors have a free constant that no clause removes.

## 3. The plan, and how it is to be read

**Read with the reset (Decision Log P264, P268).** The rules below are as they stood. Where they name cards S09 to S20, chat C10 or package N0, those cards are retired or superseded (§5, "The old cards, sorted") and the rule is read of the cards T1 to T6. Rule 11 is read with Learnings L43 and L44: every card is a test that can fail, said before it is run, and no list of meanings of an axiom is put to the PI.

**Whose plan it is.** The PI (18 November 2026, Decision Log P183): "you should decide how many work packages are , and set how many work packages are done in a particular chat.  We will follow that plan. Sometimes the chat may be short , and it may be long.  In the DAG i want you to  apply your best guestimate and we will go along with that." The work packages below, their order and their grouping into chats are therefore the architect's estimate. They are not a ruling on the theory, and no card states a result.

**The shape of the plan.** M1 took each axiom in turn, said what it means for a shape, numbered each choice, and then derived the shape (M1 v7.3, Sections 2 to 8). The plan does the same for an equation, in five parts:

- **Part I, what M2 stands on** (S01 to S05): what M1 fixes, what it leaves open, and what a state of the whole geometry is.
- **Part II, the axioms as conditions on an equation** (S06 to S12): what the equation is an equation for, then each axiom in turn, the fifth first as in M1, the sixth last.
- **Part III, the derivation** (S13 to S20): the direction of the warp, what the geometry supplies, the conditions applied, the selection, the theorem.
- **Part IV, the checks** (S21 to S24): agreement with M1 at rest, whether solutions exist, the declared input of M1, the failure tests.
- **Part V, the paper** (N1 to N5): packages that are not steps.

**Rules of the plan.**

1. **One work package is one result:** one definition, or one proposition with its proof, or one computation (`papers/M2/HANDOVER.md` §3). A chat does the work packages that §4 gives it, in order, and no others.
2. **A work package is split before it begins** if it would need more than about a dozen logged sentences, or if its reading does not fit comfortably. If it turns out larger in the doing, the chat stops at the last logged sentence and tells the PI; it is never finished by compressing it.
3. **A question on a card is a question.** Where a card names things, it names them as M1 prints them. No card says what the answer is hoped to be, and the architect has looked at no target.
4. **A step uses what its card names and nothing else** (`papers/M2/HANDOVER.md` §3). The line "May use" is the architect's estimate of what the step turns on. If a step finds that it needs a result of M1 v7.3, or an entry of the M2 log, that its card does not name, it stops, tells the PI in a sentence, and the card is amended in that chat before the result is used. External sources are entered in M2's section of Verified References first.
5. **The cards of Parts III and IV are provisional.** They depend on what S06 and S12 find. *(Since chat C7, the PI, Decision Log P232: S13 and S14 are done before S11 and S12; each is brought up to date by the chat that does it, before use, and chat C10 redraws S15 to S24.)* Chat C10 is set aside to redraw them with the results of Parts I and II in hand, and to look again at the size limit, which is first adjusted after the first two or three steps (`papers/M2/HANDOVER.md` §3).
6. **Where a step reaches a choice** the option the sixth axiom selects is taken and entered in M2's section of the Minimality Register before the tag goes on (CLAUDE.md §6). Where no clause speaks of the datum reached, the question goes to the PI.
7. **Where a step would need what a line of `papers/M2/M1_failure_tests.md` says a later paper must not need,** it is reported to the PI at once, in a sentence, with the line.
8. **Done means, for every step:** its entries are in `papers/M2/WRITE_LOG.md`, each with its place, its mark and its failure test; every term it defines is in `papers/M2/GLOSSARY.md` and in M2's section of the Definitions Register; its rows are in `papers/M2/STEP_AUDIT.md` with the red-flag scan of every source used; its finite or arithmetic parts have their script in `papers/M2/checks/` or their Lean certificate; a fresh reader has checked the chat's records against their sources; the cards after it are brought up to date. A card's own "Done means" line adds to this and takes nothing from it.
9. **Blind.** The sealed list says that the derivation, when written, is "compared with the sealed material and with the canonical form of the Master Equation": the architect takes it from this that a target exists for the equation itself (S18 to S20). For every card the PI says whether a target exists (`papers/M2/HANDOVER.md` §3); where a card says "none, as the architect takes it", that is the architect's and the PI may say otherwise. Until the PI says, every card is treated as if a target existed, and nothing is looked at.
10. **S01 and S02 are withdrawn (the PI, 18 November 2026, Decision Log P188).** M1 v7.3 is in canon word for word, and a step cites its results by printed number and TeX label, as each card's line "May use" names them; nothing of M1 is copied into the M2 log as an inventory. Where a card names "the entries of S01" or "the entries of S02" among what it may use (alone or in a range, as in "the entries of S01 to S03"), nothing of S01 or S02 is meant: the step uses the results of M1 v7.3 that its card names, and rule 4 stands. **The checklist.** `papers/M2/checks/S01_S02_quotes.md` (made by `papers/M2/checks/m1_quote.py` from `S01_S02_spec.json`; forty-two passages of M1 v7.3, cut by line) holds, in its parts S02a to S02c, the passages in which M1 itself says that something is not fixed, not shown, assumed, an input, a hypothesis, motivation, a choice or a reading. It is a working aid of the architect: not a source, not a register, not part of the paper. A step that leans on a result of M1 looks there, and then at M1 itself, to see whether M1 says the thing is not shown or is a choice. The question of card S24 on "every item of S02" is asked of that checklist. The section key `m1` is kept for whatever a later step has to quote of M1.
11. **Derive, and do not declare, where a later card can settle it (the PI, 19 November 2026, Decision Log P223, P224).** The PI: "I rather that we compute than rule it." The rule, in the words the PI confirmed as a standing rule for S09 to S12: "Where a later card can derive the answer, we wait for it and do not make a numbered choice now. A choice is made only if the derivation, once done, still leaves something open." The word "compute" is taken in the wider sense the PI confirmed: "derive from M1 and the entries, and do not declare". The architect's, for carrying it out, and not the PI's words: a card that is left open under this rule says which later card it waits for, and logs nothing until then. *(The PI, 19 November 2026, Decision Log P233: the rule applies to S13 and S14 as it does to S09 to S12.)*

12. **The trip-wires (the PI, 20 November 2026, Decision Log P270; Learnings L46).** (1) No point is put on hold: when a card is stuck, the chat says at once either the smallest computation that gives a yes or a no, or that it has no test and the point is the PI's. (2) The counter of §4 is kept by every chat; if two chats in a row record no open point decided and no candidate produced or struck, the next chat stops at its opening and tells the PI before doing anything. (3) A card is split once only; a second split is a stop, and the PI is asked whether the card is still needed. (4) Each chat begins with its own two sentences, taken from §1 and not from the handover: what stands between here and the equation, and what the chat will have decided by its end; if the second cannot be said, the chat stops. (5) A waiting point names the card that decides it and expires there: if that card ends without deciding it, it comes to the PI in that same chat. *(The architect's reading of (3), told to the PI: T1b, T4a and T4b are cards of their own, made at the PI's word (P268), and each may be split once; T1 and T4 as first drawn are not split again.)*
