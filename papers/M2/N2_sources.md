## 3. The sphere's own winding is two (CHECKED; the gap of P85)
The canonical line K of the sphere is the line of its differentials. Take the differential dz in the coordinate z. It has no zero and no pole where z is finite. In the coordinate w = 1/z at the remaining point, dz = −dw/w²: a pole of order two. The degree of a line is the number of zeros less the number of poles of any meromorphic section of it; so K has degree −2 and K⁻¹ degree 2. With S1, the integer of the determinant line of the canonical structure on S² is 2.

Used and not read at source: that the degree of a line is zeros less poles of a meromorphic section. Second route, in M1's own terms: the integer is the Euler number of the tangent bundle, and M1 has χ(S²) = 2 (l.949), as it has ∫c₂ = 3 = χ for CP² (l.683).

## 9. Third pass: Hitchin 1974 read (copy supplied by the PI)
Read: pp. 4–5 (standing hypothesis: X compact, oriented, Riemannian; H = ker P, H = H⁺ ⊕ H⁻; a Spin^c structure defines a Hermitian line L and needs a Hermitian connection on it), p. 12 (Remark (4); the canonical Spin^c structure of a complex manifold), pp. 13–14 (Theorem 2.1 and its proof; Theorem 2.2), pp. 15–16 (proof of 2.2, parts (2)–(4); Remarks (1)–(4)).

What the pages give, piece by piece (the placing on M1's pieces is the architect's):
1. **CP² and S², each with its canonical structure and no further line:** Theorem 2.1 in one statement. The proof identifies the spinors with Λ*T^{0,1}, the Dirac operator with √2(∂̄ + ∂̄*) for the Riemannian connection of a Kähler metric, and ends "The theorem follows from the Hodge theory of the Dolbeault complex". This agrees with Nicolaescu's Proposition 1.4.23 (S3).
2. **The fibre with its twist:** the torus is spin, so part (4) of the proof of Theorem 2.2 applies: for E a holomorphic Hermitian bundle with its unitary connection of type (1,0) in a holomorphic frame, "we can identify the harmonic spinors with coefficients in E with H*(X, O(L ⊗ E))", L a holomorphic square root of K. It is a sentence in a proof, not a numbered statement. On a torus K is trivial and has four holomorphic square roots (Theorem 2.2(2) with H¹(X, Z₂)); which of them M1's "one odd corner" names is not examined here. The degree of L ⊗ E is that of E for each.
3. **The whole internal piece:** not covered by one statement. CP² is not spin, so neither Theorem 2.2 nor Remark (4) of p. 12 applies to the product; Theorem 2.1 has no twist. The route remains S4 + S5 + S6 (Nicolaescu), now supported at each factor by Hitchin.
4. **Independence of the lengths, within Kähler ones (corrected: the remark is about the spin case of Theorem 2.2, which the first writing of this item did not say; the architect's error):** Remark (1), p. 16, of H^p(X, O(½K)): the cohomology "is defined entirely in terms of the complex structure on X, dim H is independent of the choice of Kähler metric defining the same complex structure." For the canonical structure without a twist the same follows from Theorem 2.1, whose right-hand side contains no metric; that inference is the architect's and is not Hitchin's sentence. This bears on §5: the count of states at rest is the same for every Kähler metric of the given complex structure, not for product lengths only; for the whole twisted internal piece that again rests on the combination of §2. For non-Kähler lengths nothing is said.
5. **A lead for S10 (not a closure):** Remark (2), p. 16 quotes Serre duality, H^p(X, O(L)) ≅ H^{n−p}(X, O(K − L)). On the fibre, K trivial and n = 1, this would turn the first cohomology of the twist into the sections of its inverse, of degree −3. Hitchin quotes the duality and is not its source; that a line of negative degree has no section is still unread. S10 stays a GAP, now reduced to these two standard statements.
