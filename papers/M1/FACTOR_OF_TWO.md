# The last factor of 2: where it is in the axioms, and a test of the half-turn connection

**10 November 2026.** Asked by the PI: "Let's go through each of the axioms and see where the last factor of 2 comes from - and is it in M1 or is this an M2 issue. then test that connection."

"The last factor of 2" is the one found in `papers/M2/ZETA_COUNT.md`: the pillowcase's own count is six, as three times two, and it becomes twelve when the fold is lifted to spinors, where a turn by π does not undo itself in two goes but in four.

**Read for this note:** M1 v2.1 ll.165–169 (Def 2.5), 298–304 (Prop 5.3), 328–336 (Prop 5.6 and its paragraph), 346 (the order-three automorphism), 436–442 (Cor 7.4). **Computed:** `checks/factor_two_check.py`, with its protocol fixed first: only the objects M1 names (the corner units i, j, k; the drive's element w), their orders, and the group they generate.

## 1. Each axiom in turn

| Axiom | What it supplies to the count | Where in M1 | A factor of 2? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A1 closure** | The turn itself: "a thing that closes on itself is a loop", and π as half a full turn at the corners | l.92; Thm 5.7 | Not a factor. It supplies the *condition*: things must come back, so the number of half-turns must be even |
| **A2 growth and decay** | The involution, which M1 finds to be the fold z ↦ −z: a turn by π. Its four fixed points make the corners | Def 2.2(iii); Prop 5.5; Thm 5.7 | **Yes, the first 2.** The fold has order two, and the doublet in each sector of the six is the four fixed points divided by that two |
| **A3 order** | The quaternions. Their units are Q₈ = {±1, ±i, ±j, ±k}; the corners are Q₈/{±1}. The three imaginary units are the three non-trivial corners | Prop 5.2; Prop 5.3 (l.299); Def 2.5 (the algebra acts on spinors) | **Yes, the last 2, and also the 3.** The {±1} is exactly the extra sign: each unit u is a half-turn of the algebra (Ad_u has order 2) but as an element has order 4, because u² = −1. And there are three such units |
| **A4 drive** | The trace-three drive: three points returned each circuit (one corner and two ordinary points); and on the algebra the element w, which carries i → j → k | Thm 4.2; Prop 4.4; Prop 5.6; l.346 | **The 3 again, with the same sign attached:** Ad_w has order 3, but w³ = −1, so w has order 6 = 3 × 2 |
| **A5 shadow** | Not in M1 v2.1 | — | none found; it does not enter the count |

**So the last factor of 2 is in M1, at the third axiom.** It is the {±1} of the quaternion units, which M1 states in Proposition 5.3 ("in bijection with Q₈/{±1}") and uses once more without comment in "w³ = −1" (l.346) and in the corner signs of Corollary 7.4. It is not an M2 issue: M2 inherits it (M2 Def 2.2: "ψ itself up to the sign ±1 that M1 Corollary 7.4 assigns to each corner"). What M1 never does is *count* with it: its twelve is 3 · 2 · 2 from ℂP², S² and the bare pillowcase.

## 2. The numbers, from M1's own algebra (all CHECKED)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Order of a corner unit i, j or k as an element | 4 (i² = −1) |
| Order of the automorphism it induces, Ad_i | 2 |
| Order of the drive's element w = ½(1 + i + j + k) | 6 (w³ = −1) |
| Order of the automorphism it induces, Ad_w (i → j → k → i) | 3 |
| Group generated by the corner units | 8 elements; 4 automorphisms |
| Group generated by the corner units and the drive's w | **24 elements; 12 automorphisms** |

The last row is M1's algebra speaking for itself. The corner automorphisms (four, counting the identity) and the drive's three-cycle together make a group of exactly **twelve** automorphisms, and each comes from exactly two elements, u and −u, making **twenty-four**. So both factorisations the PI offered are orders of groups already present in M1: 12 = 4 × 3 (four corner automorphisms, the drive's three) = 3 × 2 × 2; and the further 2 is the sign.

## 3. The test of the connection

**The connection proposed:** the drive returns two ordinary points of the pillowcase with a half-turn (`papers/M2/checks/count_blind.py`, C1), and a half-turn is exactly what takes a spinor four goes to undo. So the π per unit and the spin's factor of 2 may be one fact.

**The test.** Count, for each thing that returns, how many half-turns it takes before a spinor carried with it is back to itself.

| What returns | The turn at each return | Half-turns until a spinor closes | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A corner unit u (the fold at a cycled corner, M1 Prop 5.6) | a half-turn of the algebra | **4** (u has order 4) | CHECKED |
| An ordinary returned point (M₀z = −z) | a half-turn of the torus about the point, each circuit | **4** (two returns make a full turn, which gives a spinor −1; four make it +1) | the half-turn CHECKED; "a full turn gives a spinor −1" is the same u² = −1 |
| The three cycled corners under the drive | a third of a turn each circuit | twice round the three: **6** steps, a total of two full turns = 4 half-turns' worth of angle | CHECKED (w has order 6) |

**Result as I first wrote it, now withdrawn in part (see §5; the error was mine):** "the connection holds. In every case the spinor closes after a total turning of two full turns." What survives the blind check is narrower: the half-turn on return and the spin's factor of 2 are one algebraic fact, that the spin lift of a turn by π has order four. The three rows of the table are not the same kind of count, and they do not add.

**The arithmetic that follows, stated and not claimed as a derivation.** Three things are returned each circuit (the trace of the drive). Each needs four half-turns for a spinor to close. Three times four half-turns is twelve half-turns: 12π. Factor by factor:

- **π**: one half-turn, the angle of the fold (A2);
- **× 2**: two half-turns make a full turn, which closes a loop (A1);
- **× 2**: a spinor needs two full turns (A3, the {±1});
- **× 3**: three things returned by the least drive (A4, trace three).

## 4. What this is and is not

- **Disclosure.** I knew the target was 12π. The protocol of the script was fixed first and every number is reported, including the 24 and the 18 of the earlier check; but the arrangement in §3 was written by someone who knew what it should add up to. It must be treated as a candidate until a blind check has gone over it.
- **There are now three twelves, and they are not known to be the same.** (i) M1's χ(K₈) = 3 · 2 · 2, from ℂP², S² and the bare pillowcase. (ii) The sector count of the pillowcase with the spin lift, 3 × 2 × 2 (`ZETA_COUNT.md`). (iii) The twelve automorphisms generated by the corner units and the drive (§2). Three ways of reaching one number is a reason for more caution, not less, until it is shown whether they are one fact.
- **It is not a derivation of ζ.** It is a count of total turning. Still open: why the coupling in the field equation *is* that total turning; why the three add; and why a turning of 12π is seen as a growth of 12π.
- **The first row of §3 and the second are different kinds of "three".** The three cycled corners are met in succession; the three returned points are there every circuit. The arithmetic of §3 uses the returned points. Which is meant by "three generations" is the PI's to say (see record §13, "1 + 2 shadows").

## 5. Independent blind check (measure 3) — done 10 November 2026

**How it was run.** A fresh sub-agent was given the setting and eight numbered claims (the facts of §2, the three rows of §3, and the steps "the same count" and "therefore a total"), with no files and no statement of what total was hoped for.

| Claim | Blind verdict | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| The drive returns three points of the pillowcase, 1 + 2 | follows (recomputed by hand, with a Lefschetz cross-check) | stands |
| Orders 4, 2, 6, 3; group of 24 with 12 automorphisms | follows (recomputed by hand) | §2 stands |
| At an ordinary returned point, "a half-turn each time; closes after four" | **needs substantial hypotheses; "closes after four" does not follow as worded.** The return map there is a stretch combined with a half-turn; only the half-turn part closes; the stretch never does. And the 4 depends on declaring the double cover: "the closing count … is 1 in PSL(2,ℝ), 2 in SL(2,ℝ), 4 in Mp(2,ℝ)" | row 2 of §3 holds only for the half-turn part, under a declared choice |
| At the corner P₁, "likewise four" | the statement about going round a cone point follows; **"likewise" does not.** "K3 is dynamical: it counts iterations of the drive. K4 is holonomy: it counts circuits round a point." At P₁ the drive supplies no half-turn at all | rows are different kinds of count |
| The three cycled corners "are" the element w with w³ = −1 | **does not follow; it is an identification.** The same automorphism has two lifts, w (cube −1) and −w (cube +1); "the sign is a choice of lift". And the one natural map from the drive to the 24-element group sends the drive to an element of order 4, not 6 | row 3 of §3 is an identification, and a doubtful one. Re-checked: M₀² = [[5,3],[3,2]] ≡ −I modulo 3, so modulo 3 the drive has order 4 |
| "Each of the three needs four half-turns" | **does not follow.** "Either criterion applied uniformly gives (4,4,1) or (1,1,4), never (4,4,4)" | the "same count" of §3 is withdrawn |
| "The total is three times four half-turns" | **does not follow.** No reason for a sum; it "adds unlike units". The two genuinely additive theorems available give other numbers: the total cone deficit is 4π (Gauss–Bonnet), and the Lefschetz sum is 2 | the arithmetic of §3 is withdrawn |
| Is any total singled out? | "Twelve is cheap." It lists eight routes to 12 from the small numbers present, and totals of 4, 16, 18, 24 and 64 by equally natural rules. "The setting forces only the lcm (4) and the two additive theorems (4π; 2)." | — |

**What is established, in the blind check's words and mine.** The facts of §§1–2. That the spin lift of a turn by π has order four, which is the single fact behind both the half-turn on return and the factor of 2. **Not established: any total over the returned points, and in particular not twelve.**

**So §1 stands and §3's conclusion does not.** The last factor of 2 is in M1 at the third axiom, as the {±1} of the quaternion units. The arithmetic "three returned things, four half-turns each, twelve half-turns" was mine, was written knowing the target, and does not survive: the three counts are of different kinds and nothing says they add.

**A constraint for whatever comes next.** A total such as 12π can only be the value of something *additive*: an angle accumulated along one path, an index, an exponent of a product. The two additive quantities this geometry is known to have are the total cone deficit, 4π, and the Lefschetz number, 2. A derivation of ζ has to name the additive quantity it is computing before it counts anything.
