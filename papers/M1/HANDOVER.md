When time has gone once round its circle, the torus that arrives is the torus that set out, redrawn by the drive.
     >
     > A state of the world is a moment together with a point of that moment's torus. Its path goes round the circle of time, and at each moment it sits somewhere on the torus of that moment. This path is the helix. It is not drawn on the torus, and it is not drawn on the circle of time. It runs through the two together, and since the circle has one dimension and the torus two, the space it runs through has three.
     >
     > When the helix has gone once round, time is back where it began, and the point on the torus, for a typical state, is not. Every dawn is a dawn, and no dawn is the one before.
     >
     > The picture to avoid is that of time itself swollen into a doughnut, with the helix wound on its surface. In that picture the torus contains the time direction. Here it does not: time is the ring, and the torus rides round it.
