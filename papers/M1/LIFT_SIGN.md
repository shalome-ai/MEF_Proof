# The sign of the lift: is the drive's element w or −w?

**10 November 2026.** Directed by the PI: "take the sign of the lift". It decides between a period of three circuits (12π) and six (24π) in `papers/M2/ZETA_LEAD.md`.

**Disclosure.** The wanted answer (period three) was known to me. The protocol of the script was fixed before it ran; the steps were given to a blind checker without the target; its counterexample was re-run here.

**Read:** M1 v2.1 ll.165–169 (Def 2.5), 264–272 (Prop 4.4 and the note on orientation), 299–304 (Prop 5.3), 328–336 (Prop 5.6), 346, 436–442 (Cor 7.4), 602. **Computed:** `checks/lift_sign_check.py`.

## 1. What M1 says

M1 says the drive acts on the quaternions "as the order-three inner automorphism Ad_w, w = ½(1 + i + j + k)" (l.329) and records w³ = −1 (l.346). The automorphism is the same for w and −w. **M1 never says which element the drive is, only which automorphism**; and nowhere uses the value of w³. (READ.)

## 2. What the two signs are, as turns (CHECKED)

| Element | As a turn of i, j, k | Three circuits | Six circuits |
|---|---|---|---|
| w | a third of a turn, 2π/3 | 2π: a spinor has −1 | 4π: +1 |
| −w | the same position reached the long way, 4π/3 the other way round | 4π: a spinor has +1 | — |

So the question is whether the drive turns i, j, k by a third of a turn each circuit, or by two thirds the other way.

## 3. The computation

M1 has only the fold and the drive, and on those alone both signs are allowed (blind check Q1; the group they generate has no relation that could fix a sign). A sign can be fixed only by asking the lift to respect more of the torus's symmetries: the quarter-turn S (which M2 uses in its exchange τ) and the shears.

| # | Step | Blind verdict |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | The symmetries of the torus are generated by the quarter-turn S and the sixth-turn U = ab, with S² = U³ = fold | follows |
| L2 | The drive is M₀ = U⁻¹ S U⁻² S | follows (CHECKED) |
| L3 | The drive is a product of commutators (it is a·b⁻¹, and a, b count alike) | follows |
| L4 | Ask for elements s, u of the unit quaternions, for S and U, respecting those relations, and acting on i, j, k **exactly** as the symmetries act on the three cycled corners (through modulo 2) | follows, "but only because of" the word *exactly*, which "is a choice, and it carries the whole result" |
| L5–L7 | Then the drive is −w (or −w⁻¹): cube +1 | follows (CHECKED: script, both cases) |
| L8 | "So the period on spinors is three" | does not follow without stated reasons for L4 |

**The counterexample to going further (blind check Q3; re-run and CHECKED).** There is another assignment, in the 48-element group, with s = (i − j)/√2 and u = ½(1 + i − j − k). It respects the same relations, gives the drive as **+w exactly**, cube −1, turning i → j → k exactly. It differs in that the sixth-turn U turns i, j, k with signs (i → −j), and a shear has order 8 where in the first assignment it has order 4. A third assignment (modulo 3, the 24-element group) sends the drive to an element of order four, which does not cycle i, j, k at all.

## 4. Result

**The sign is not decided by M1.** It is decided by a condition M1 does not state:

- **If every symmetry of the torus acts on the quaternions only through what it does to the corners** (so that a symmetry which leaves all four corners where they are leaves i, j, k alone), **the drive is −w**: it turns by 4π/3 each circuit, three circuits make 4π, and a spinor is back to itself after three. Period three.
- **If symmetries may also change the signs of i, j, k**, the drive can be w, and the period on spinors is six.

The first condition is the principle M1 itself gives for the corner algebras: "the surviving algebra at a point is what its stabiliser fixes" and the drive "carries the lines with the points" (l.332): the algebra is moved by moving corners, and by nothing else. On that principle the sign is −w. But M1 states the principle for the fold and the drive, not for the shears and the quarter-turn, so extending it is a step and is marked as one.

**Correction to M1 implied (if the condition is adopted).** M1's "w³ = −1" (l.346) is true of the element M1 wrote down and is not the drive's element: the drive's is −w, with cube +1. The certificate `L2_w_cubed` certifies the arithmetic of w, which is unaffected. Nothing else in M1 uses the sign. Not applied to the text; proposed.

## 5. What this does to the ζ count

Under the condition, "three spins to get back to unity" holds for spinors as well as for i, j, k, and the 24π alternative falls away. Still needed, unchanged: the closure sentence (`papers/M2/ZETA_LEAD.md`, last section, item 2); why the coupling of Φ is this quantity; the unit of length.

**An observation, not a claim.** With −w the drive turns i, j, k through 4π over the three circuits. 4π is also the total angle missing at the four corners (M1 Thm 5.7). Whether these are one fact is not shown, and after today's blind checks a coincidence of small numbers is the default assumption.

## 6. Failure test

If some symmetry of the torus that fixes all four corners is shown to act non-trivially on i, j, k (for instance through M2's exchange τ or the Spin^c corner signs of Cor 7.4, where three corners carry +1 and one −1), the condition fails and the period on spinors may be six. Cor 7.4's signs are the first place to test this: they are signs attached to corners, which is exactly what the condition forbids for i, j, k.

**What it shows.** Both turns exist as assignments; neither is excluded by M1. The corner signs of Cor 7.7 do not make the condition of §4 fail: the failure test of §6 does not fire under the link of A3. But the link is the condition of §4 in a stronger form (A4), so A3 is not independent evidence for three. **The sign is not decided by M1's axioms and definitions; it is a choice between an assignment in a group of 12 with three steps and one in a group of 48 with six.**

**Failure test.** A later paper that needs a symmetry fixing every corner to act on i, j, k; or a reason that only the fold and the drive are assigned elements (h1 dropped), in which case the two signs differ by nothing but the number of steps.
