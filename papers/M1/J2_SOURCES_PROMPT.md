# Prompt for a separate chat: sources for J2 (written 11 November 2026; Decision Log P99)

The PI gives the text between the lines to a Claude chat with web access. What comes back is saved, unedited, as `papers/M1/J2_sources_from_chat.md`. **Standing of what comes back:** a report by another session. A statement in it is not READ until the session working on J2 has read the page itself, from a copy the PI saves into the repository or supplies; until then it is "reported". The prompt names nothing of the project.

---

I need exact statements from the published literature on dynamical systems, for a referee-style check of a paper. Please work from the sources themselves and not from memory.

**Rules for your answer**
1. For every statement, quote it **verbatim**, with the theorem or proposition number, the page number, and the source (author, title, journal or publisher, year). Include **every hypothesis** in the statement exactly as printed (for example: compactness, dimension, orientability, smoothness class, any condition on the non-wandering set, codimension).
2. Say for each quotation **where you read it**: give the URL or DOI of the copy you actually opened. If you could only see a secondary source that restates the result (a survey, a textbook, lecture notes, a later paper), say so, quote the secondary source verbatim with its own page and number, and say plainly that the original was not read.
3. If you cannot open a source, say "NOT ACCESSED". Do **not** reconstruct a statement from memory, and do not paraphrase in place of a quotation. If you add anything from memory, put it in a separate final section headed "FROM MEMORY, NOT VERIFIED".
4. For each source you found in an openly and legally hosted copy (author's page, arXiv, a journal's free archive, a library archive), give the link, so that I can download the PDF myself.
5. Do not summarise or interpret beyond what is asked. Where two sources state a result with different hypotheses, show both and say what differs.

**What I need**

A. **J. Franks, "Anosov diffeomorphisms", Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 14 (1970) 61–93.** The exact statement(s) on: (i) which compact manifolds of dimension two carry an Anosov diffeomorphism; (ii) conjugacy of an Anosov diffeomorphism to a hyperbolic linear automorphism of a torus. With every hypothesis, in particular any assumption on the non-wandering set and on codimension.

B. **S. Newhouse, "On codimension one Anosov diffeomorphisms", Amer. J. Math. 92 (1970) 761–770**, and **J. Franks, "Anosov diffeomorphisms on tori", Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 145 (1969) 117–124**: the main theorem of each, verbatim, and what each adds to A. Also **A. Manning, "There are no new Anosov diffeomorphisms on tori", Amer. J. Math. 96 (1974) 422–429**: the main theorem.

C. **A. Katok and B. Hasselblatt, *Introduction to the Modern Theory of Dynamical Systems*, Cambridge University Press, 1995.** The exact statements, with numbers and pages, of:
 (i) the topological entropy of a hyperbolic linear automorphism of the two-torus (or of the n-torus) in terms of its eigenvalues;
 (ii) the periodic points of a hyperbolic linear automorphism of the torus (that they are the points with rational coordinates), and anything said about their measure and density;
 (iii) the definition of an Anosov diffeomorphism used in the book, and the statement that a hyperbolic linear automorphism of the torus is Anosov;
 (iv) the book's statement of the classification of Anosov diffeomorphisms of tori or of surfaces, if it has one;
 (v) the definition of topological entropy for a flow, and any statement relating the entropy of a suspension flow (mapping torus flow) to that of the map.

D. **A question, to be answered only from a citable source.** Let S be a closed orientable surface and f a diffeomorphism (or homeomorphism) of S with positive topological entropy and whose set of periodic points has Lebesgue measure zero. Is S forced to be the torus? Specifically: is there a published construction of a pseudo-Anosov map on a closed surface of genus two or three obtained as a lift of a hyperbolic torus automorphism to a double cover of the torus branched at points of a finite invariant set, and commuting with the covering involution? Quote the construction or theorem verbatim (candidates to look in: Fathi–Laudenbach–Poénaru, *Travaux de Thurston sur les surfaces*; Farb–Margalit, *A Primer on Mapping Class Groups*; Thurston, "On the geometry and dynamics of diffeomorphisms of surfaces", Bull. AMS 19 (1988)). State: the genus of the cover, the number of branch points, the number of fixed points of the covering involution, the entropy of the lift, and whether the lift is smooth or only a homeomorphism at the branch points. If no source states this, say so.

E. **One elementary fact, with a citable statement:** that a linear map of the plane preserving a lattice and preserving a positive-definite quadratic form has finite order (equivalently, that a hyperbolic element of SL(2, Z) preserves no flat metric on the torus). A textbook statement with page is enough.

**Format of the answer:** sections A to E; under each, one numbered item per statement, in the form: source · number and page · "verbatim quotation" · where read (URL/DOI) · ORIGINAL READ / SECONDARY ONLY / NOT ACCESSED. Then the list of links to open copies. Then, last, "FROM MEMORY, NOT VERIFIED", if anything.
