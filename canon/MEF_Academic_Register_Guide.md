# The MEF Academic Register: A Style Guide Derived from Three Model Papers

**Purpose.** This document distils the linguistic, rhetorical, and structural conventions that a career mathematical physicist recognises instantly as "one of us." It is derived from close reading of three model papers, each representing a slightly different sub-register:

- **Hori & Tong**, *Aspects of Non-Abelian Gauge Dynamics in Two-Dimensional N=(2,2) Theories* (hep-th/0609032) — the theoretical high-energy physics register: field theory prose, physical arguments woven through calculations, results-first framing.
- **Bruinier & Funke**, *On Two Geometric Theta Lifts* (math.NT/0212286) — the automorphic-forms / arithmetic-geometry register: definition-theorem-proof spine, adjointness statements, "the point is here that…"
- **Zwegers**, *Mock Theta Functions* (Utrecht thesis, arXiv 0807.4834) — the analytic number theory / thesis register: patient historical framing, proposition-by-proposition development, unhurried exposition of a new theory.

The MEF corpus sits at the intersection of these three: geometric unified field theory (Tong-Hori territory), theta-lift and Weil-representation machinery (Bruinier-Funke), and mock modular / K₈ partition function content (Zwegers). Papers in each MEF sub-domain should tilt toward the corresponding model, but the shared register below applies universally.

---

## 1. The Overarching Register

### 1.1 The "one of us" test

A career academic recognises insider writing not by any single feature but by the *simultaneous absence of tells*: no overclaiming, no ornament for its own sake, no undue enthusiasm, no explanation of things the reader already knows, no failure to explain things a knowledgeable reader would not know, no colloquial connective tissue ("so basically", "what's really cool is"), no bulleted structure where prose is proper, no visual formatting (bold, coloured boxes) doing work that prose should do. Each of the three model papers reads as if the authors are quietly explaining something they understand very well to peers who will follow at the same pace.

The dominant emotional register is **understated confidence**. Claims are stated flatly. Difficulties are acknowledged in passing. Novelty is announced without adjectives. There is no "remarkably", "strikingly", "profoundly" unless the object of the sentence genuinely warrants it and even then sparingly. Zwegers, describing his own thesis contribution to a question posed by Zagier, writes only: *"We find elliptic and modular transformation properties for these functions."* That is the tone.

### 1.2 The first-person plural, and when it drops

All three papers use **"we"** as the default authorial voice, even Zwegers writing alone. This is the mathematical-physics convention and it is non-negotiable in the MEF corpus. Reserve "the author" and "I" for acknowledgements and prefaces only.

"We" carries several distinct duties which authors move between without ceremony:
- **Editorial "we"** — "We now give a summary of the main results." (Tong-Hori, p.2)
- **Inclusive "we"** (author with reader, walking through a calculation) — "We first show that the Coulomb branch is lifted for generic masses…" (Tong-Hori, p.15)
- **Programmatic "we"** — "The purpose of this paper is threefold." (Bruinier-Funke, p.1)
- **Historical / attributional "we"** — "In this chapter we first study a function h, which is essentially the function ϕ studied by Mordell." (Zwegers, p.5)

Occasional impersonal constructions ("It is easily checked that…", "One obtains…", "The point is here that…") appear at the highest-tension moments to remove the writer from the proof. Overuse of "one" reads as translated German and should be occasional.

### 1.3 UK English, capitalisation, and typographic discipline

All MEF papers use UK spelling per author preference: *behaviour, analyse, generalisation, colour, favour, centre, organisation, modelling, factorisation, parametrisation*. This is consistent with Bruinier-Funke and Tong (Cambridge). Zwegers, writing in Utrecht, is more mixed but leans UK.

Mathematical objects are capitalised sparingly. Named theorems (**Cauchy's theorem**, **Serre duality**, **Liouville's theorem**, **Poisson summation**) are capitalised only for the name; generic categories ("theorem", "proposition", "lemma", "definition", "remark") are lowercase in prose but capitalised at the start of the environment. Common nouns for mathematical objects — moduli space, gauge theory, modular form, theta series, quadratic form, symmetric space — are lowercase in prose.

Displayed equations are numbered by section (1.1), (2.3), etc., not run-through. Punctuation follows equations as if the equation were a noun clause — comma, semicolon, or full stop as required. Bruinier-Funke: *"we prove that ξₖ defines an antilinear map ξₖ : Hₖ → M!₂₋ₖ, to the space of meromorphic modular forms of weight 2 − k which are holomorphic on H (see Proposition 3.2)."* The equation display and its post-punctuation are treated as one sentence.

## 2. How Papers Open

### 2.1 The title

Titles in this register are **descriptive noun phrases**, not questions and not slogans:

- *Aspects of Non-Abelian Gauge Dynamics in Two-Dimensional N=(2,2) Theories*
- *On Two Geometric Theta Lifts*
- *Mock Theta Functions*

MEF paper titles should follow this template. Prefer:
- *The Icosian Completion of K₈*
- *A Mock Jacobi Form on the Spinᶜ Compactification K₈*
- *On the Topological Derivation of the CKM Matrix*

Avoid the sales-pitch title (*A New Unified Framework for Everything from First Principles*) and the question title (*Can Consciousness Be Geometric?*). Note the "On …" and "Aspects of …" gambits: they signal a contribution within a settled field.

### 2.2 The abstract

Abstracts in this register have four recognisable moves, in order, executed briskly:

1. **The setting**, in one sentence. *"We study various aspects of N=(2,2) supersymmetric non-Abelian gauge theories in two dimensions, with applications to string vacua."*
2. **The specific results**, enumerated in flat declarative sentences. *"We compute the Witten index of SU(k) SQCD with N > 0 flavors with twisted masses; the result is presented as the solution to a simple combinatoric problem. We further claim that the infra-red fixed point of SU(k) gauge theory with N massless flavors is non-singular if (k, N) passes a related combinatoric criterion."*
3. **What the results are applied to.** *"These results are applied to the study of a class of U(k) linear sigma models which, in one phase, reduce to sigma models on Calabi-Yau manifolds in Grassmannians."*
4. **A single sentence identifying the strongest single downstream consequence.** *"This proves a mathematical conjecture of Rødland."*

Abstracts do not contain: exclamation, adjectives of praise for the theory, forward references ("as we shall see"), or the phrase "in this paper we". Length is 100–200 words. No bullet points. No equations (Bruinier-Funke's abstract has none; Tong-Hori has none). Zwegers has no abstract because it is a thesis, but the "This thesis" subsection performs the same duty.

**MEF template for an abstract:**

> We derive [specific object] from [geometric input] on the manifold M₁₂ = (S¹_Sol × S³) × K₈, χ(K₈) = 12. The construction proceeds by [one-clause mechanism]. We obtain the numerical value [X], to be compared with the experimental value [Y]; the deviation is [Z]%. As an application we [downstream consequence]. The result is [Tier classification, in one restrained clause about its evidential weight].

### 2.3 The introduction

All three papers open Section 1 with a sentence of the form "**The purpose of this paper is …**" or an equivalent framing. This is not a stylistic accident; it is the accepted first move, and it should be adopted throughout the MEF series. Compare:

- Tong-Hori: *"The purpose of this paper is to study the quantum dynamics of two dimensional N=(2,2) supersymmetric gauge theories with non-Abelian gauge groups."*
- Bruinier-Funke: *"The purpose of this paper is threefold: (1) … (2) … (3) …"*
- Zwegers, "This thesis": *"This thesis is the result of my research on the following two questions, both posed by Don Zagier: 1. How do the mock ϑ-functions fit in the theory of modular forms? …"*

Immediately after this framing, the introduction does four things in order, usually across 3–5 pages:

1. **Motivation from prior literature.** Cite the specific works that motivated the question. *"Our motivation for this work arose from a number of mathematical conjectures concerning the moduli spaces of Calabi-Yau manifolds embedded in Grassmannians [1, 2]."* Note the flat naming of the antecedent; no ceremony.

2. **Statement of the main results, one by one, with subsection headings in bold or roman.** Tong-Hori uses labelled paragraphs: **The Witten Index**, **IR Dynamics of SU(k) Gauge Theories**, **Splitting the Conifold Singularity**, **The Glop Transition**. Bruinier-Funke states Theorems 1.1 through 1.5 inside the introduction, in numbered form. Adopt whichever fits the paper; the MEF main derivation papers should state their results as numbered Theorems/Propositions inside the introduction (Bruinier-Funke pattern), and the wider survey papers should use labelled paragraphs (Tong-Hori pattern).

3. **A concise indication of the method or the shape of the argument**, so the reader knows in advance what technical machinery is being deployed. This is short — one paragraph — and forensic in style. Bruinier-Funke: *"We show this by switching to the Fock model of the Weil representation. Then the idea for the proof of Theorem 1.2 is given by the following formal (!) calculation: … The first equality follows from the adjointness of the raising and lowering operator. However, the second scalar product no longer converges and needs to be regularized."* Notice the "(!)" — a rare mark of controlled emphasis, used exactly once and with reason.

4. **A one-paragraph "paper is organised as follows" map**, with section numbers. This is expected; do not omit it. Bruinier-Funke: *"The paper is organized as follows. After setting up the basic notions in section 2, we discuss in section 3 the space of weak Maass forms Hₖ in detail and prove Theorem 1.1. In section 4, …"*

### 2.4 What introductions do *not* contain

- No motivational appeal to significance ("this could revolutionise…"). If the reader needs to be told the result matters, the abstract has failed.
- No historical exposition beyond what is needed to state the antecedent. Zwegers is the exception because his thesis introduces mock theta functions to a wider audience; he opens with the Ramanujan-Hardy letter of 1920. MEF papers that introduce a novel construction to a specialist audience should be closer to Tong-Hori — cite the antecedent, state the problem.
- No definitions. Definitions belong in Section 2.
- No unmotivated forward references. Every "we shall see below" should point to a specific numbered result.
- **No mention of AI tools, computational aids, or writing process.** These belong exclusively in the acknowledgements paragraph with the exact wording specified in §7.

## 3. The Anatomy of a Section

### 3.1 Section openings

Every technical section opens with a **short orienting paragraph** — typically two to five sentences — that says what the section will do and, ideally, how it will do it. This is a soft parallel to the introduction's "paper is organised as follows", localised to the section. Examples:

- Tong-Hori §2.2: *"Consider a U(1) gauge theory with N chiral multiplets of charge Qᵢ, i = 1, …, N. The criterion for conformal invariance is ΣᵢQᵢ = 0. Since the location of the singularities in the Kähler moduli space does not depend on the complex structure moduli, we may perform the analysis for the situation with vanishing superpotential and the corresponding non-compact Calabi-Yau."*
- Zwegers Ch. 2, §2.1: *"The classical theta series associated to a positive definite quadratic form Q : Rʳ → R … is the series Θ(z; τ) := Σ_{n∈Zʳ} e^{2πiQ(n)τ+2πiB(n,z)}. These theta series have well-known transformation properties. In particular Θ(0; τ) is a modular form of weight r/2. In [11] Göttsche and Zagier define a theta function for the case when the type of Q is (r − 1, 1). … In this chapter we give a modified definition."*

Note the pattern: a known object is recalled in one sentence, its known properties in another, the previous partial attempt is identified, and then the sentence that positions this section's contribution is delivered — often beginning "In this chapter we…" or "The purpose of this section is…".

### 3.2 Definition–Proposition–Proof structure

For any MEF paper doing genuine mathematics (as opposed to a survey), the workhorse structure is:

- **Definition** (numbered, in an environment)
- **Proposition / Theorem / Lemma** (numbered, in an environment; the statement is a full mathematical sentence and stands alone)
- **Proof.** — beginning immediately, delimited by "**Proof:**" and closed by "□" or the end of the paragraph.
- **Remark** or continuation prose, only where needed.

Zwegers's exposition is the cleanest model. Proposition 1.2 states six numbered properties of the Mordell integral h(z), and the proof enumerates them (1), (2), (3), … in the same order, each in a compact paragraph. The reader can always find where each claim is proved. Do not fold proofs into flowing prose in MEF derivation papers; separate them explicitly.

The corresponding technical vocabulary inside proofs is compact:
- "Note that …"
- "It follows that …"
- "Using [X], we find …"
- "By [Y] we have …"
- "We conclude that …"
- "This last integral is well known and equals …"
- "Letting z₀ vary over a fundamental parallelogram … and m, n vary over Z, we see that f(z) is bounded and tends to 0 as Im(z) → ∞, so f ≡ 0 by Liouville's theorem."

The proof is a chain of small justified steps; it never appeals to something like "clearly" without giving the appeal. When a step is easy, the standard mark is "It is easily checked that …" or "One sees at once that …" — these are permitted, but each of them still names *what* is easy.

### 3.3 Physical arguments (Tong-Hori style)

For MEF papers where the argument is not a formal proof but a physical derivation (Papers II, III, V, VI, VIII in the corpus), the register shifts to what Tong-Hori exemplify: **claims are stated with the same flatness, but the connective tissue between them is physical reasoning rather than logical deduction, and the standard of rigour is different.**

The characteristic sentence type is: *"In the regime r ≫ 0, the manifold X appears, sitting at the bottom of the scalar potential of this theory."* Or: *"To see whether the Coulomb branch survives in the quantum theory, we compute the exact potential energy in the large |σ| region of the field space. All charged matter is heavy and may be safely integrated out, resulting in an effective twisted superpotential …"*.

Physical arguments are **signposted with reasoning verbs**: *see, find, conclude, deduce, propose, expect, argue, check, verify, note, observe, recall.* The register never elides the reasoning; each transition tells the reader why the next step follows. When an approximation is used, it is named ("the Born-Oppenheimer approximation"), and any risk it introduces is acknowledged briefly ("This mass gap validates the second stage Born-Oppenheimer approximation").

Where the argument depends on a claim not fully proven, this is stated directly and unemphatically: *"We propose that the SU(k) theory suffers from such a singularity if and only if there exist k distinct N-th roots of unity that sum to zero."* Notice: **"We propose"**, not "we prove"; not "we conjecture" either — "propose" is the correct mid-strength verb for a strongly-motivated but non-rigorous claim. In MEF papers, use the graded vocabulary [R]/[D]/[M]/[G] rigour tags in tables and lists; in prose, use "we prove", "we derive", "we compute", "we show" for [R]/[D]; and "we argue that", "we propose", "we identify", "we observe" for [M]/[G]. Never inflate the verb.

### 3.4 Section closings

Sections close with **a single short paragraph** that either states the conclusion or hands off to the next section. Tong-Hori §5.3 ends: *"We thus conclude that the low energy theory at r ≪ 0 is the non-linear sigma model whose target space is the rank A(p) = 4 locus in CP⁶, that is, the Pfaffian variety Y. The Pfaffian Y is a Calabi-Yau threefold and hence the resulting conformal field theory has ĉ = 3 as expected. In this manner, the strongly coupled non-Abelian gauge theory has two phases in which the low-energy physics is described by a sigma-model on inequivalent Calabi-Yau manifolds, X and Y. This confirms the conjecture of Rødland."*

Notice the closure: a technical conclusion, a consistency check ("ĉ = 3 as expected"), the wider statement ("two phases … inequivalent Calabi-Yau manifolds"), and finally the connection back to the framing question ("This confirms the conjecture"). This four-beat closure is worth adopting as a template for MEF section endings.

## 4. Voice at the Sentence Level

### 4.1 The declarative default

The default sentence in all three papers is a **flat declarative** that states a mathematical fact or a physical claim. There is no hedge, no signposting, no persuasion. Bruinier-Funke: *"For q = 2, it is well known that D is Hermitian."* One sentence, one fact, one full stop. Move on.

Compound and complex sentences are used freely, but the complexity is always in the mathematical content, not in the syntax:

Tong-Hori: *"For all non-zero finite values of r, the classical vacuum equation V = 0 results in a Higgs branch of vacua corresponding to a non-compact Calabi-Yau manifold while the vector multiplet scalar is forced to vanish: σ = 0."*

The colon after "vanish" is doing important work: it makes the equation the noun completion of "is forced to vanish". This is a very common construction in the register and should be adopted freely in MEF papers where an equation follows naturally from a verb phrase.

### 4.2 Connectives to have on tap

The connective tissue between sentences and paragraphs in this register is remarkably narrow. The following short list covers almost every case:

*In particular. Moreover. Furthermore. Hence. Thus. It follows that. Note that. Observe that. Indeed. On the other hand. However. Nevertheless. Consequently. In fact. To see this, … For example. That is, … We claim that … We now turn to … Recall that …*

These do the work that in less controlled writing gets done by "so", "basically", "at the end of the day", "clearly". Prefer *"To see this, we compute …"* over *"Now let's compute this to see what happens."*

**Words to avoid** because they are register-alien: *awesome, powerful, mind-blowing, incredibly, absolutely, essentially* (as a filler; permitted as "essentially the function ϕ studied by Mordell"), *arguably, ultimately, at the end of the day, basically, obviously* (as filler; permitted only when what follows is truly obvious). *Cool* is fatal. *Deep* is dangerous unless applied to a specific technical property (a deep result, i.e., one hard to prove from first principles) and even then rare.

### 4.3 Signposting novelty and difficulty

Novelty is announced quietly. The standard is Tong-Hori: *"To our knowledge, the calculation for SU(k) theories has not appeared in the literature."* Or Bruinier-Funke: *"Hence from this aspect the lift Φ(z, f, ψ) and its features are new."* Novelty is a factual claim about the state of the literature; it is not a boast.

Difficulty is acknowledged in passing, usually to justify a choice of technique. Bruinier-Funke: *"However, the second scalar product no longer converges and needs to be regularized. At this point, one also obtains an 'error' term involving the Kähler form. … An additional difficulty occurs by the fact that in the intermediate steps one also has to deal with singularities along the special divisors."* The paragraph flags three separate technical obstacles in a single passage, each in half a sentence. Compare with the counter-register that MEF papers should avoid: *"This is really hard because there are singularities everywhere. But don't worry, we handled it."*

### 4.4 Meta-remarks

Occasional light meta-remarks are permitted and give the paper personality without breaking the register. Zwegers, on the Fourier transform: *"We see that g is its own Fourier transform! (Note the unusual plus sign in the definition of the Fourier transform)."* The single exclamation, the parenthetical caveat: this is how a mathematician talks to another mathematician who might otherwise be tripped up by a convention. Use sparingly. One or two per paper.

Watson's aside quoted by Zwegers — *"as a friend remarked to me a few months ago, I am an old-fashioned mathematician"* — is the extreme case of what is allowed in the register. In MEF papers, comparable asides could be used at the transition between sections, never mid-derivation.

## 5. Presenting Mathematical Content

### 5.1 Introducing an equation

The register has a small set of standard patterns for introducing an equation. The MEF Master Equation should be introduced using pattern (a) below, augmented with the definition box mandated by the terminology corrections.

**(a) The equation as the completion of a definitional or derivational sentence.**
Tong-Hori: *"we can integrate out all massive fields, leaving only the gauge multiplets for the unbroken U(1)ᵏ, to obtain the effective superpotential: [display equation (3.6)]."*
Zwegers: *"For z ∈ C and τ ∈ H set [display equation defining h(z)]."* — Definition 1.1.

**(b) The equation as a stated identity or transformation law.**
Zwegers: *"we then have Θ(γτ, ϕ, L) = φ(τ)ʳ ρ_L(γ, φ) Θ(τ, ϕ, L);"* — no preamble, the equation is the sentence.

**(c) The equation as the summarising conclusion of a paragraph.**
Tong-Hori: *"Thus the truly flat Coulomb branch appears when [display equation (2.12)]."*

Pattern (a) is the correct choice for MEF papers when introducing the Master Equation because the equation is being *defined* into existence for the reader.

### 5.2 The Master Equation, in-register

Here is a model presentation combining pattern (a) with the terminology mandates. This is the phrasing MEF papers should use verbatim on first introduction in each major section (paraphrased in subsequent references):

> Combining the Einstein contribution from the M₁₂ curvature with the dilaton kinetic term derived in Section [X], we obtain the Master Equation:
>
> [display]  G_{MN} = 8π G_{eff} T_{MN} + ζ ∇_M Φ ∇_N Φ + Λ_{eff} g_{MN}     (1.1)
>
> Here:
>
> - G_{MN} is the Einstein tensor of the twelve-dimensional metric g_{MN};
> - T_{MN} is the matter stress-energy tensor;
> - ζ ∇_M Φ ∇_N Φ is a rank-2 symmetric tensor constructed as the outer product of covariant derivatives of the **Quantum Coherence Dilaton** Φ = ω(ψ − ψ̄)², where ω denotes the consciousness coherence field, ψ and ψ̄ the quantum and anti-quantum spinor condensates localised at the Q-wall and AQ-wall respectively, and (ψ − ψ̄)² the associated scalar chiral asymmetry;
> - ζ = 12π is the quantum coherence coupling constant, whose value is derived topologically in Paper [X];
> - Λ_{eff} g_{MN} is the effective cosmological term arising from the Q / AQ vacuum imbalance;
> - Φ itself is not consciousness but encodes the coupling between the consciousness field ω and the chiral asymmetry (ψ − ψ̄)².

Note the register: definitions are lettered items, not bullet-tinted call-outs; the phrase "Quantum Coherence Dilaton" is set in bold on first occurrence only; and the equation is numbered by section. In subsequent references within the same section, one may use "the dilaton kinetic term ζ ∇_M Φ ∇_N Φ" without repeating the full expansion, and after the term is established across the paper, "the dilaton" alone will suffice — but the full expansion must reappear at the start of any new major section.

The phrase to be used in prose for the second term is *"quantum coherence coupling gradients contribute to curvature"* or *"the dilaton kinetic term sources spacetime curvature"*. **Do not write** "consciousness gradients create curvature" or any variant conflating ω with Φ.

### 5.3 Equations inside sentences vs. displayed

Displayed equations are used for objects that will be referred to again, or that carry the main content of the paragraph. Small algebraic manipulations — "since σ is traceless, Σ_a σ_a = 0" — stay inline. When in doubt, err toward displaying: the density of displayed equations in Tong-Hori and Zwegers is high, and this signals to the reader that the paper is technical.

### 5.4 Notation and macros

All three model papers use a **stable notation once introduced** and never redefine a symbol without warning. This is the reason for the MEF `mef_macros.tex` mandate: no local redefinitions, no shadowing. If a paper needs a symbol used differently, either flag it explicitly ("in this section only, we write φ for the density rather than the scalar field") or choose a different symbol.

Where a symbol has a name — Φ is the **Quantum Coherence Dilaton**, ω is the **consciousness coherence field**, ζ is the **quantum coherence coupling constant** — the name should appear on first mention *in each paper of the series* and thereafter be replaceable by the symbol. This matches Zwegers's treatment of "the Mordell integral" (named on first mention, subsequently h(z)), and Bruinier-Funke's treatment of "the Kudla-Millson lift", "the Borcherds lift", "the weak Maass form" — each is coined in the introduction, given its symbol, and used symbolically thereafter.

The `\Q` = ℚ (rationals only) convention should be enforced everywhere; any older paper that used ℚ or 𝒬 for the dilaton must be corrected. This is a hard house rule.

### 5.5 Tables

Both Tong-Hori and Zwegers include tables. Tong-Hori Table 1 is a two-dimensional integer array of Witten indices — a clean, uncaptioned numerical table with a one-line title. Adopt this format for MEF numerical prediction tables. Do not use coloured cells, alternating row shading beyond what a journal style file provides, or emoji. Table captions are one sentence, sentence case, with a full stop.

## 6. Rhetoric of Novelty, Rigour, and Provenance

### 6.1 The MEF-specific challenge

The MEF corpus makes claims of unusual weight — derivation of Standard Model parameters, cosmological constants, and mock modular structure from a single 12-manifold. In this register, weight of claim demands *further* restraint in prose, not more. The reader who sees an extraordinary result stated modestly reads it with more credit than one where the author is straining to be believed. Compare:

- **Wrong register:** *"Remarkably, our framework derives the fine-structure constant to seven decimal places with zero free parameters — a result unprecedented in the history of physics."*
- **Right register:** *"The topological derivation of Section 4 yields α⁻¹ = 137.03599… (see Table 2), to be compared with the CODATA value α⁻¹ = 137.035999084(21). The deviation, [X] parts per [Y], is discussed in Section 5."*

The second version does not need adjectives. The numbers do the work. If they are wrong, adjectives will not save them; if they are right, adjectives are noise.

### 6.2 The deflationary reading

A convention established across the MEF corpus, and matching best mathematical practice, is that after any strong result one should state the **deflationary reading** explicitly. In the icosian programme paper, the sentence *"The icosian ring 𝕀 is the unique maximal order in Hamilton's quaternions over ℚ(√5)"* is offered as the honest deflationary framing of the E₈ derivation. This move belongs in every MEF paper where the result could be misread as more than it is. It is the hallmark of an author who has thought carefully about what has and has not been shown, and it disarms an obvious line of hostile reading.

The register for the deflationary reading is a paragraph beginning "**One should be careful about the interpretation of [X].**" or "**We note the following deflationary reading.**" or "**It is important to distinguish what has been shown from what has not.**" The paragraph then states, in flat sentences, exactly what the argument does and does not establish.

### 6.3 Rigour tags in prose

The MEF corpus uses the tags [R] (rigorously derived), [D] (derived), [M] (model-dependent), [G] (goal / gap). In prose, these appear inside brackets after the relevant claim, or as annotations in tables. Do not overuse them within a single paragraph — one or two per page is sufficient. Where an entire paragraph makes model-dependent claims, a single "[M]" at the start of the paragraph is acceptable.

The Bruinier-Funke analogue is the distinction between "we prove", "we show", "it is well known", and the softer "we hope to come back to these issues in the near future" for open problems. Every MEF paper's conclusion or outlook section should include at least one such honest handing-off of open questions.

### 6.4 Citing prior work

Citations are numbered in square brackets [3, 9, 12] and appear in the sentence naturally, not as ornament. Zwegers: *"In [3] Andrews gives most of the fifth order mock theta functions as Fourier coefficients of meromorphic Jacobi forms."* The citation is grammatically part of the sentence. Bruinier-Funke: *"The Borcherds lift was generalized by the first named author and studied in connection with the cohomology of the special divisors [Br1, Br2]."*

In LaTeX use `\cite{PaperV}` (no space, current bibkey — see memory for the old→new mapping). Never write "as shown in [15]" as a hanging phrase without saying what was shown; either integrate the citation into a full clause ("as shown by Kudla-Millson [15]") or drop it into the middle of a sentence where the grammar carries the content.

Prior MEF papers should be cited by their numeric bibkey (`\cite{PaperV}` etc.) with a short indication of the result being drawn on: *"as derived in Paper V \cite{PaperV}, the Weinberg angle admits the topological expression …"* — never bare citations without a lead-in.

## 7. Acknowledgements and the AI-Tool Statement

The acknowledgements paragraph closes the introduction or sits at the very end of the paper, before the bibliography. The MEF-mandated wording is:

> The author used Claude (Anthropic) and Gemini (Google DeepMind) as mathematical development and analytical tools during the preparation of this work. All intellectual content, theoretical framework, and scientific claims are the sole responsibility of the author.

This is the only place in the paper where Claude or Gemini appears. Do not write "I used Claude to help me derive this" in the main text, do not credit AI in captions or footnotes, and do not describe the workflow inside the paper. The statement above is complete, sufficient, and in-register.

Beyond the AI statement, thank collaborators and hosts in the Bruinier-Funke pattern: *"This project was first conceived and advanced during three visits from 1999 to 2002 of the second named author in Heidelberg. He would like to thank the Forschergruppe Arithmetik at Heidelberg and E. Freitag in particular for their generous hospitality during these visits. … We also would like to thank J. Burgos, E. Freitag, W. Kohnen, S. Kudla, and J. Millson for useful discussions and comments on this project."*

This is the correct register for personal acknowledgements: warm, specific, and without inflation. Do not thank an AI here; the AI-tool statement stands alone.

## 8. Footnotes

Footnotes in this register are rare, technical, and short. They handle: a convention statement ("Throughout the paper, we use the convention of [16, 11] in which the action is multiplied by 1/2π in the exponent of the path-integral weight." — Tong-Hori footnote 1), a private communication ("This consequence was first brought to our attention by Edward Witten (April, 2005)." — Tong-Hori footnote 41), or a caveat that would break the flow of the main argument. Do not use footnotes for exposition; if it needs a paragraph, it belongs in the main text.

## 9. Register Differences Across the Three Models — When to Lean Where

**Papers deriving numerical predictions from geometric input** (Papers II, III, IV, V, VI, VIII, IX, XVI in the corpus) should lean **Tong-Hori**: physical reasoning is the connective tissue, results are announced up front, an "outlook / applications" section closes the paper. The abstract lists what has been computed. Section headings are noun phrases naming physical objects ("The Witten Index for Two Dimensional SQCD", "IR Dynamics", "The Glop Transition"). Adopt the equivalent for MEF: "The CKM Matrix from S¹_Sol Monodromy", "The Cosmological Constant from K₈ Vacuum Energy".

**Papers establishing mathematical structure** (the Icosian Completion paper, Paper XI on Mock Modular K₈, Paper XV on the Geometric Origin of Mock Modularity, Paper XX on Almost Quaternionic Structure) should lean **Bruinier-Funke**: theorem–proof spine, adjointness and duality statements, "the point is here that …" — the paper reads as a theorem paper, and its main results are numbered Theorems in the introduction. The abstract enumerates the theorems. Section headings are technical noun phrases ("Basic Notations", "Weak Maass Forms", "The Fock Model and the Schwartz Forms").

**Long-form / thesis-style papers** (the eventual monograph, or extended derivation papers that develop machinery before applying it) should lean **Zwegers**: a patient historical introduction, chapters rather than sections, propositions numbered by chapter, and an unhurried development in which each chapter opens by recalling the classical setting before stating the new definition. The "This thesis" section replaces or supplements the abstract.

Every MEF paper, regardless of which model dominates, must obey the shared register described in §§1–8.

## 11. A Short Checklist for MEF Paper Drafts

Before circulating any MEF paper, run through the following. If any answer is "no", revise.

1. Is the title a descriptive noun phrase, not a question or slogan?
2. Does the abstract state (i) setting, (ii) results, (iii) applications, (iv) principal downstream consequence, in that order, without adjectives of praise?
3. Does Section 1 open with "The purpose of this paper is …" or a direct equivalent?
4. Does the introduction contain a "the paper is organised as follows" paragraph pointing to the sections by number?
5. On first introduction, is the Master Equation presented with the full expansion Φ = ω(ψ − ψ̄)², explicit ζ = 12π, and lettered definitions of every term?
6. Are ω, ψ, ψ̄, Φ, ζ used consistently throughout the paper, with no local redefinitions and no conflation of Φ with consciousness?
7. Is `mef_macros.tex` inputted in the preamble and no macro redefined locally?
8. Is UK spelling used throughout?
9. Are rigour tags [R]/[D]/[M]/[G] applied where appropriate, and does the paper state, at least once, the deflationary reading of its main result?
10. Are all citations of prior MEF papers using the current bibkeys (PaperII, PaperIII, etc.) and integrated grammatically into sentences?
11. Does the acknowledgements paragraph use the exact AI-tool wording specified in §7?
12. Is the paper free of: exclamation marks (except at most one for genuine emphasis, Bruinier-Funke style); bullet points in prose; visual boxes doing work that prose should do; adjectives of praise; and any language that would break the register at a career-academic read?

If all twelve answers are yes, the paper is in-register.
