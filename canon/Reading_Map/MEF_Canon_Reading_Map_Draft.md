## How to read this Map

The canon is a series of papers, M1, M2, M3 and so on. Each paper uses only the papers before it and ordinary published mathematics; nothing is taken on trust from the forty papers that came before the series. Every paper carries machine-checked certificates for the parts of its argument that are finite or arithmetic, so that anyone can download them and have a computer confirm them. Every paper names, in one place, the points at which it took the smallest option the axioms allowed, so that a reader who disagrees with a choice knows exactly which results depend on it.

Each entry below has the same five parts. *The question* says in plain words what the paper is trying to find out. *Why it comes here* says what it needs from the papers before it. *What it proves* gives the result in words first and a formula second. *What you can check* names the certificates. *What is asked of peer review* says which step a referee should push on hardest, because that is the step most likely to be wrong. *How this links to what we already know* names the discoveries of the great mathematicians and physicists that the paper contains — because the claim of this canon is not that it replaces them but that they were never pieces of different puzzles. Einstein's spacetime, Kaluza and Klein's extra dimensions, Hamilton's quaternions, Arnold's cat map, Ramanujan's mock theta functions, Dirac's operator, Riemann's zeta: each was found separately and each is forced here by the same four sentences. One picture, not many; the canon's name for it is the Shape of Becoming.

The Map is written for a reader in the first year of a mathematics or physics degree. If you have met a derivative, a vector and the idea that a shape can have holes, you have what you need. Where a word is unavoidable it is explained the first time.

## How it began

It began the way such things sometimes do: with a scribbled intuition about a unified field theory, followed by frantic work to write it down. That first paper was almost entirely motivated rather than proved — it said what ought to be true and why it would be beautiful if it were. It led to nearly forty more papers, each fixing some of the previous one and adding its own promissory notes, until it became clear that the right thing to do was to stop, keep the four sentences the whole edifice had been resting on, throw away everything that had not been derived, and start again with Paper M1. This canon is that restart. The forty papers are its sources, not its authorities.

## The four axioms

Everything in the canon follows from four sentences a child could check, read as conditions on a shape, with one rule for choosing between shapes.

**Axiom 1 — Closure.** The universe closes on itself, so that it can be aware of itself.

**Axiom 2 — Growth and decay.** The universe grows and decays as a function of itself.

**Axiom 3 — Order.** The order of events matters, so that each instance is unique and cannot be foretold.

**Axiom 4 — Drive.** The universe is in constant change; it never stands still.

**The standing principle.** Wherever the axioms leave a choice, take the smallest option — the one that adds nothing the axioms did not force. Whenever the smallest option fails, say so where it fails. Every paper names each time it used the principle.

## The constants hiding in plain sight

Before the first paper, a word about the numbers you will meet, because you already know all of them. Every schoolchild meets π, the ratio of a circle to its width. Everyone who has watched money compound or a population grow has met e, the number that growth produces when it feeds on itself. The golden ratio φ turns up in sunflowers, in the Parthenon and in the Fibonacci sequence 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8. The imaginary unit i, the square root of minus one, is the first thing a first-year learns that "cannot exist" and then uses every day; its three-dimensional cousins i, j, k — Hamilton's quaternions — are the numbers that describe how things rotate in space. And Euler's identity, e^{iπ} + 1 = 0, is routinely called the most beautiful equation in mathematics because it ties five of these together in one line and nobody can quite say why.

In this canon they are not decorations. Each of them is forced by one of the four axioms, and each appears exactly where the axiom does.

The second axiom, growth and decay as a function of itself, is the sentence that *defines* e: a quantity whose rate of change is proportional to itself grows as e raised to a power. The warp of the shape is an exponential, and the amount by which it falls across half the pillowcase is 12π. The fourth axiom, the drive that never stands still, forces the smallest chaotic map of a torus onto itself — the one mathematicians call Arnold's cat map, because Arnold illustrated it by stretching and folding a picture of a cat — and that map stretches by exactly φ² each circuit. The golden ratio is the rate of the universe's clock. The third axiom, that order matters, forces the quaternions i, j, k as the algebra of the fibre, and the four corners of the pillowcase are labelled by 1, i, j, k; the three imaginary units are the three generations of matter and the three colours of the strong force, seen from two sides. And π is the angle missing at each corner of the pillowcase: a torus folded in half has four points where a full turn of 2π has become a half-turn of π, and that missing π is the number that multiplies the Euler characteristic 12 to give the coupling ζ = 12π.

Euler's identity is where they meet, and the canon gives it a meaning. The operation that exchanges growth and decay is a quarter-turn of the fibre together with a reversal of the drive; a quarter-turn is multiplication by i. Done twice, it is multiplication by i² = −1 = e^{iπ}: the fold of the torus that makes the pillowcase. So e^{iπ} = −1 is, in this canon, the statement that two exchanges of growth and decay are one fold of the shape — the exponential of growth, the imaginary unit of order, and the π of the corners, in one line, and that line is proved in M2 (the certificate `L4_S_sq_fold`). The constants everyone learns at school are the fabric of the shape, and they have been in plain sight the whole time.

## The shape of the canon

The series moves outward in rings. The first two papers derive the stage and the one rule of motion on it: the shape of the universe and its field equation, which turns out to be Einstein's equation with one additional term. The next four do the mathematics that everything after them consumes: the spectrum of the shape, the shadow that every projection casts, the algebra of its corners, and the one number that fixes its proportions. Then the known physics, in the order the geometry produces it: the quarks and their mixing, the leptons and the Higgs, the cosmological constant, black holes, Hawking radiation, the strengths of the forces and the weak mixing angle, the fine-structure constant, the Higgs mechanism. Then the physics that is usually called esoteric — the collapse of the wavefunction, the dark matter ratio, the earliest galaxies — which here is not esoteric at all but the same geometry read in its shadow sector. Then biology and organic chemistry, which few people know are physics: why life is left-handed, why mitochondria are built as they are, why mirror-image molecules behave as they do in medicine. Then the mathematics that the whole programme rests on, Hilbert's programme, which this canon argues should never have been abandoned. And finally three of the Millennium Prize problems, which — if the shadow sector had been known a century ago — would have been seen as the same problem three times, each with a comparatively direct solution once the completion is in hand.

One historical curiosity is worth stating at the front. The first number the framework ever produced, in that scribbled first paper, was the weak mixing angle. In the canon it is the last of the known-physics results to be derived, because it turns out to depend on everything else. That reversal is the measure of the distance between the first paper and this one.

# Part I — The stage and the rule

## M1 — Four axioms and their minimal geometry

**The question.** If the universe must close on itself, grow and decay as a function of itself, have events whose order matters, and never stand still — what shape must it have? Not what shape could it have: what shape it must have, if no shape is allowed to carry anything the four sentences did not force.

**Why it comes here.** It uses nothing but the four axioms, ordinary geometry, and the rule of taking the smallest option. It is the beginning.

**What it proves.** There is exactly one such shape, and it has twelve dimensions. It is built from a circle of time wound with a particular twist (the Sol geometry of the Fibonacci drive), a three-dimensional sphere for space, and an eight-dimensional internal part made from a complex projective plane, an ordinary sphere, and a pillowcase — a torus folded in half, with four sharp corners. The paper proves that the drive must be the smallest hyperbolic one (trace three, growth by the square of the golden ratio each circuit), that the dimension must be a multiple of four and at least twelve, that the internal part must carry a particular spin structure, that the pillowcase has exactly four corners labelled by the real numbers and the three imaginary quaternion units, and that the whole shape has Euler characteristic twelve. It has one input the axioms do not fix: its overall size.

In symbols: M₁₂ = (S¹_Sol × S³) × K₈, with K₈ = [(ℂP² × S²) ×_w (T²/ℤ₂)]^{Spin^c}, χ(K₈) = 12.

**What you can check.** Five certificate files, forty-nine theorems: the drive, the fibre and its corners, the dimension, the base and its spin structure, the scale sector.

**What is asked of peer review.** Four readings — the points at which a plain word became a mathematical condition — are numbered and should be tested one by one. The step most likely to be contested is the second reading, which identifies the involution of growth-and-decay with the fold of the torus; M2 refines it. The minimality criteria themselves are the paper's hypotheses and a referee should ask whether a different ordering of them would select a different shape.

**How this links to what we already know.** Einstein's spacetime is inside this shape: the block S¹ × S³ — a circle of time with a three-sphere of space — is the geometry of Einstein's own first cosmological model of 1917, here forced rather than chosen. The internal part is Kaluza and Klein's idea of 1921 and 1926, that the forces are the shape of extra dimensions, carried through: ℂP² × S² × S¹ is exactly the seven-dimensional space Witten showed in 1981 is the smallest that carries the Standard Model's symmetry, and M1 recovers Witten's result as a corollary. The twist of time is one of Thurston's eight geometries of the 1970s, the drive is Arnold's cat map of the 1960s, the corner algebra is Hamilton's quaternions of 1843, and the Euler characteristic is Euler's of 1758. None of them is new. What is new is that one set of four sentences forces all of them at once.

## M2 — The Master Equation from four axioms

**The question.** On that shape, what rule of motion do the same four sentences impose? And how does it compare with the equation Einstein wrote in 1915?

**Why it comes here.** It uses M1's shape and nothing else.

**What it proves.** The rule of motion is Einstein's equation with one additional term. Two of the axioms — closure and order — give Einstein's equation with its matter term and its cosmological constant. The axiom Einstein's theory does not contain, growth and decay, gives the additional term: the growth law has two faces, growth and decay, which the paper shows are one field ψ and its mirror ψ̄; the even combination ω = ψ + ψ̄ is the Coherent Field, and the one scalar built from them that respects the mirror and vanishes both when the faces coincide and when growth exactly opposes decay is the dilaton Φ = ω(ψ − ψ̄)². The fourth axiom, the drive, makes the two faces genuinely two rather than one face reflected: the operation exchanging them is the reversal of the drive together with a quarter-turn, it reverses the orientation of the shape, and so ψ and ψ̄ have opposite handedness. That is the arrow of time and the distinctness of matter from its shadow, as one fact. The strength of the new term, ζ = 12π, is the amount by which the geometry's warp falls across half the pillowcase: Euler characteristic times the missing angle at a corner.

In symbols: G_{MN} = 8πG T_{MN} + ζ T^Φ_{MN} + Λ g_{MN}, ζ = χ(K₈)π = 12π.

**What you can check.** Seven certificate files, fifty-five theorems: the parity classification, the flow components, the integers of ζ, the quarter-turn and the eigen-directions, the warp exponent, the shape of ω, the Clifford module.

**What is asked of peer review.** The fifth reading (the metric is positive-definite; time is the drive's orbit parameter), and the three minimality choices that fix the action: one gravitational term, the induced spin connection with no free coupling, and the geometry coupling to the sheets through Φ alone. The normalisation κ₂² = 2/L in the derivation of ζ is tagged as the paper's one undischarged step.

**How this links to what we already know.** The left-hand side is Einstein's of 1915, obtained the way Hilbert obtained it the same year, by making an action stationary; Lovelock's theorem of 1971, that in four dimensions nothing else is possible, is here extended by the minimality principle to twelve. The matter term is Dirac's of 1928. The additional term is a scalar coupled to gravity, the object Jordan, Brans and Dicke studied in the 1950s and 1960s and Kaluza–Klein theory produces automatically as the size of the extra dimensions; the stiff equation of state the term obeys along the flow is Zel'dovich's of 1961. The warp that fixes ζ is the mechanism Randall and Sundrum found in 1999. Each of these was a separate discovery. Here they are consequences of one another.

# Part II — The mathematics everything else consumes

## M3 — Which axioms survive

**The question.** The forty earlier papers accumulated twenty axioms. How many of them does the canon actually need?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M1 and M2 to know what has already been proved.

**What it proves.** Of the twenty, most are theorems of M1 and M2; several are targets to be proved in later papers of this canon, each assigned its owing paper; three are not axioms at all but descriptions of what the equation will later show about what can pass between our four dimensions and the other eight; and at most one is genuinely independent — the Shadow Principle, that every singularity in our four-dimensional world is the projection artefact of something smooth in twelve, and that the smooth completion is unique. The paper attempts to derive that principle from the first axiom, closure; if the derivation closes, the canon rests on four axioms and nothing else, and if it does not, it rests on four axioms and one, with the reason it cannot be derived from inside stated honestly.

**What you can check.** Certificates for the finite parts: the corner and wall bookkeeping, the parameter count, the uniqueness of the two-to-one lift at a corner.

**What is asked of peer review.** The uniqueness step in the derivation of the Shadow Principle. Existence of a completion is nearly free once the shape is closed; uniqueness is the hard part.

**How this links to what we already know.** The question is Euclid's and Hilbert's: how few assumptions, and which? Hilbert axiomatised geometry in 1899 and asked for the same of all mathematics; Gödel in 1931 showed what an axiom system cannot do from inside. M3 asks the same question of a physical theory and finds that the answer is four sentences and possibly one more — and that the one more is exactly a statement about completion from outside, which is where Gödel's result lives. Occam's razor of the fourteenth century is the standing principle, made precise.

## M4 — The spectrum

**The question.** Every shape has a set of natural frequencies — the notes it would ring with if struck. What are the notes of the twelve-dimensional shape, and in particular of its four sharp corners?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M1's shape and spin structure and M2's field ω, whose meaning it completes.

**What it proves.** The natural frequencies of the Dirac operator at the corners are the whole numbers, each with a multiplicity equal to the number of odd divisors of the number; the sum over them is a known object from number theory, the square of the Riemann zeta function with one factor removed; and the partition function of the shape — the sum over all its states — is a mock Jacobi form, an object whose modular symmetry fails by a precise, computable amount. The paper also completes M2's account of the Coherent Field: ω is zero when the shape's frequencies are rigidly ordered, zero when they are uncorrelated, and largest in between, in the regime of strong level repulsion.

**What you can check.** The spectrum at the corners, the divisor counts, the zeta identity, the Fourier coefficients of the partition function.

**What is asked of peer review.** The bridge theorem between the field ω of M2 and the spectral functional; and the claim that the partition function is mock rather than fully modular, which is the fact every later paper of the shadow sector depends on.

**How this links to what we already know.** Fourier in 1807 showed that a shape's behaviour is the sum of its natural frequencies; Dirac in 1928 found the operator whose frequencies describe electrons; Weyl in 1911 and Kac in 1966 asked whether you can hear the shape of a drum. The frequencies found here are whole numbers with multiplicities from number theory, and their sum is Riemann's zeta function of 1859 squared — Riemann's function appearing as the sound of a geometric shape, which is what Hilbert and Pólya guessed a century ago it ought to be. The partition function is a mock Jacobi form, the kind of object Ramanujan wrote to Hardy about in 1920 without being able to define, and the statistics of the frequencies are those Wigner found for nuclei in the 1950s and Montgomery and Dyson found for the zeros of zeta in 1973.

## M5 — The shadow

**The question.** When you project a smooth twelve-dimensional object into four dimensions, something is lost. What exactly is lost, and can it be recovered?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M4's spectrum and partition function and M2's mirror operation.

**What it proves.** The amount by which the partition function fails to be modular — what number theorists since Zwegers have called the shadow — is exactly the part of the shape's intrinsic torsion that the projection discards. The operator that extracts the shadow is the differential of the fold. The sign structure of the shadow is the monodromy of the drive. And at the four corners the shadow vanishes identically — the Fixed-Point Vanishing theorem — so that what is lost at a corner is recovered non-locally from the rest of the shape. The Shadow Principle of M3 becomes, for the pillowcase, a theorem.

**What you can check.** The torsion computation, the sign table of the monodromy, the vanishing at the four corners.

**What is asked of peer review.** The identification of the Bruinier–Funke operator with the Tate differential of the fold; it is the hinge between the geometry and the number theory.

**How this links to what we already know.** Ramanujan's last letter to Hardy in 1920 described functions that almost had modular symmetry and failed by a precise amount; it took until Zwegers in 2002 to say what the failure — the shadow — was, and Bruinier and Funke in 2004 to find the operator that extracts it. The torsion that the shadow turns out to be is Cartan's of 1922, the quantity Einstein and Cartan debated in their letters as a possible completion of general relativity. The cohomology that organises the fold is Tate's of the 1950s. Ramanujan's mock theta functions and Einstein–Cartan torsion were never in the same conversation. They are the same object seen from analysis and from geometry.

## M6 — The algebra

**The question.** The four corners carry four algebras — the real numbers and three copies of the complex numbers. What happens when you build a tower of such things to infinity?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M1's corner algebras and M2's mirror operation, which turns out to be the transpose.

**What it proves.** The infinite tower is the hyperfinite factor of von Neumann, with a real structure fixed by the transpose; quaternionic decorations of it are unique up to conjugation; and the classes of the corners are the dyadic rationals — fractions whose denominators are powers of two — with the trace equal to the corner index. It converges with M5 on the Fixed-Point Vanishing theorem from the other side.

**What you can check.** The tower's dimensions, the transpose relations, the dyadic classes.

**What is asked of peer review.** The rigidity theorem — that the decorations are conjugate within the real form — against the classification literature.

**How this links to what we already know.** Von Neumann and Murray classified the infinite algebras of operators in the 1930s and 1940s and found a unique 'hyperfinite' one; Connes in the 1970s classified its automorphisms and won a Fields Medal for it. The real structure fixed by the transpose is the one Størmer and Giordano studied from the 1970s. The corner classes being dyadic rationals — halves, quarters, eighths — ties the algebra to the simplest fractions a child meets. The algebra of the corners of a folded torus and the algebra of quantum observables are one structure.

## M7 — The one proportion

**The question.** The internal shape has two radii, for the projective plane and the sphere. What fixes their ratio?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M1's scale sector and M2's module-valued dilaton, whose trace supplies a weighting that earlier derivations had assumed.

**What it proves.** The ratio τ₂ = R_w/R_c is the positive root of an integer cubic, 6u³ + u² − 72u − 4 = 0 with u = τ₂², and equals 1.8466. The two flux quanta that feed the cubic are three and six, from the spin structure and the index theorem. The paper also settles whether the empty twelve-volume has an energy of its own — the earlier corpus assumed none — by showing what the closure condition actually requires. The pillowcase's own aspect ratio, formerly confused with τ₂, is shown to move with the drive and is renamed.

**What you can check.** The cubic and its root, the flux quanta, the thirty-two-fold weighting, the drive's corner cycle.

**What is asked of peer review.** The index-matching step that fixes the second flux quantum at six, and the scale-fixing sector that places the radii at the stationary point.

**How this links to what we already know.** Kepler in 1619 believed the proportions of the solar system were fixed by harmony; they were not, but the idea that a proportion can be forced by a quantisation condition is Dirac's of 1931, when he showed that the existence of a single magnetic monopole would quantise all electric charge. # Part III — The known physics, in the order the geometry gives it

## M8 — Quark masses and the CKM matrix

**The question.** Why are there three generations of quarks, why do their masses span five orders of magnitude, and why do they mix in the particular way the CKM matrix records?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M2's warp and walls, M4's zero modes, and M7's proportion.

**What it proves.** The three generations are the three cycled corners of the pillowcase; the masses are overlaps of zero modes localised at those corners, separated by the half-period displacement; the hierarchy is the warp exponent of M2 acting across the base; and the mixing angles follow from the geometry of the corners, with the CP-violating phase the spin holonomy accumulated across the ψ-axis.

**What you can check.** The overlap integrals, the displacement geometry, the holonomy phase.

**What is asked of peer review.** The zero-mode assignment to corners and the normalisation of the overlaps; this is where the corpus had most of its fudges, and the canon's derivation must be read with that history in mind.

**How this links to what we already know.** Cabibbo in 1963 found that the weak force mixes quarks by a small angle; Kobayashi and Maskawa in 1973 showed that three generations allow a complex phase and hence the violation of CP symmetry, and predicted the third generation before it was found. Yukawa's couplings of 1935, which give fermions their masses, are here overlap integrals, as Arkani-Hamed and Schmaltz proposed in 2000 for fields localised at different points in an extra dimension; the exponential hierarchy is Randall and Sundrum's warp. The three generations are three corners of a folded torus, and the mixing angles are the geometry of the corners.

## M10 — The cosmological constant

**The question.** Why is the energy of empty space so absurdly small — one part in 10¹²² of its natural scale — and yet not zero?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M2's equation and M7's proportion.

**What it proves.** The vacuum energy is sequestered: the warp of M2 falls by 12π across half the pillowcase, and the volume it weights is suppressed by e^{−8ζ} = e^{−96π}, which is the right order of magnitude with no free parameter. The paper settles the Einstein-frame weight that earlier versions left ambiguous and derives the four-dimensional constant from the twelve-dimensional one fixed in M7.

**What you can check.** The exponent arithmetic, the warped-volume integrals.

**What is asked of peer review.** The frame in which the vacuum energy is measured; a factor of e^{2A} either way is the whole result.

**How this links to what we already know.** Einstein added the cosmological constant in 1917 and later called it his greatest blunder; Zel'dovich in 1967 showed that quantum field theory predicts a value 10¹²⁰ times too large; Weinberg's 1989 review named it the worst prediction in physics. Randall and Sundrum's warp of 1999 showed how an exponential can bridge a hierarchy. Here the exponent is the Euler characteristic of the internal shape times π, and the suppression is the warped volume.

## M11 — The Schwarzschild interior

**How this links to what we already know.** Schwarzschild found the first exact solution of Einstein's equations in 1916, within weeks of the equations' publication, while serving on the Russian front. The Shadow Principle says the singularity is the projection of something smooth, which is the picture Kaluza–Klein lifts of black holes — Gross and Perry, 1983 — gestured at without a reason to prefer one lift.

## M13 — The gauge couplings and the weak mixing angle

## M14 — The fine-structure constant

**The question.** Why is the electromagnetic coupling 1/137?

## M15 — The Higgs mechanism

**The question.** Where does the Higgs potential — the Mexican hat — come from?

**Why it comes here.** It needs M7's radii and M9's identification of the Higgs field.

**What it proves.** The two-dimensional sphere in the internal shape cannot give a Higgs mass by the naive route (its integrated curvature is a topological invariant and cannot vary); the true mechanism is the competition between the bulk tension that resists deforming the sphere and the quantised flux whose energy falls as the sphere grows, which together produce the Mexican hat with its two coefficients in terms of the radii; the vacuum value and the Higgs mass follow.

**What you can check.** The Gauss–Bonnet obstruction, the two coefficients.
