# Abstract guidelines — the anomaly-first anatomy

**Status:** PI-ruled this session. The register guide's 100–200 word band for abstracts is **removed**; the governing limit is the **target venue's** (JTA: 300; record per venue at submission). All other register rules stand (no praise adjectives, no bullets, attribution-correct verbs). This note is staged for PI banking; the corresponding one-line amendment to `MEF_Academic_Register_Guide.md` §on-abstracts is proposed, not self-banked.

**Provenance:** distilled from two worked instances — Paper 3 (*The Zwegers Shadow as U(1)-Projected Intrinsic Torsion*) and Paper C (*Equivariant Dirac traces…*, abstract as of `_v7`, preferred over the inventory form by all three round-3 referees).

---

## The anatomy, in order

1. **The anomaly couplet.** Two standard facts, parallel syntax, one sentence each or one sentence jointly. Ideally from two traditions; at minimum from two regimes (finite/limit, local/global, algebraic/geometric). Close the couplet with a short flat claim about the gap — "no result in the literature connects them" (Paper 3) or "each statement is classical" + what this paper does to the pair (Paper C).
2. **The surprise.** One sentence carrying the main theorem with hypotheses complete enough to be checkable. This sentence is the abstract's centre of mass; everything else serves it.
3. **Mechanism texture.** One clause or sentence naming the proof's most striking mechanism or object — the thing a reader will remember ("a chiral telescoping at the corners leaves one unpaired doublet… the cube of the Dedekind eta function"; "the transpose-fixed corner symmetry anchors the tower").
4. **Deflation, in the abstract itself.** One honest limit of the result, stated plainly, not deferred to a remark ("the failure of modularity lives in the projection"; "realisation is one-directional: towers of every admissible degree share the same decorated limit"). This is the house signature: the abstract polices its own boundary.
5. **Concrete closer.** A specific payoff — a number, a pinned constant, a named unique object ("the odd determinant class pins the lowest toroidal Dirac eigenvalue to $\pi$"; "the limit transpose is, up to conjugacy, the unique involutive antiautomorphism of the factor").

## The two tests before banking

**The editor test.** Simulate the *specific* managing editor of the target venue reading only the abstract. If the couplet is one an expert in that field deflates in a single thought (Paper C's first-draft couplet — trace values in $[0,1]$ versus integer indices — is just $K_0(R)\cong\mathbb{R}$ to an operator algebraist), it reads as manufactured and is worse than no couplet. Pick the couplet the editor can verify from memory but whose resolution he cannot: for JTA, the finite two-class / limit one-class involution dichotomy passed where the trace/integer pair failed.

**The rhyme test (concealment).** Before banking, diff the draft against every sibling island abstract for shared constants, shared distinctive vocabulary, and shared closing moves. Two island abstracts that rhyme verbatim hand a reader of both a linkage the concealment register denies. Worked instance: Paper 3 closes on "the anticanonical three of $\mathbb{CP}^2$"; Paper C's abstract therefore says "the canonical spin$^{\mathrm{c}}$ structure" and never the word "anticanonical", although the constant is the same object in another role.

## Verb discipline in abstracts

Attribution-correct verbs bind in the abstract exactly as in prose: "we prove/derive/compute/show" only for results proved in-document; results resting on a cited classification take "we show, by the classification of …" or "we identify". An abstract has no citations, so this is the only place attribution can live. (Caught twice in the Paper C cycle; both instances were the drafting assistant's, both corrected pre-review.)

## Where to find the couplet

The couplets are usually already in the paper, wearing other clothes: look in the deflation remarks, in any corollary named "tension" or "resolution", and in the finite-stage/limit contrasts. Paper C's winning couplet sat verbatim in its own dichotomy remark; the losing one sat in its "K-theoretic tension" corollary. Prefer the one whose two halves the paper *proves or exhibits*, over the one it merely cites.

## Worked examples

**Paper 3 (couplet + closer):** *"An almost quaternion-Hermitian manifold fails to be quaternion-Kähler exactly when its intrinsic torsion is non-zero; a harmonic Maass form fails to be modular exactly when its shadow is non-zero. Each is standard within its tradition; no result in the literature connects them."* → […] → *"…the odd determinant class pins the lowest toroidal Dirac eigenvalue to $\pi$."*

**Paper C, `_v7` (couplet + deflation-closer):** *"On a complex matrix algebra of even size there are exactly two conjugacy classes of involutive antiautomorphisms, the orthogonal and the symplectic; on the hyperfinite type II₁ factor there is one. Each statement is classical; this paper exhibits the collapse along a geometric tower."* → […] → *"Realisation is one-directional: towers of every admissible degree share the same decorated limit."*

**Anti-example (rejected in the Paper C cycle):** opening with the construction — "We realise the hyperfinite type II₁ factor as the limit of a tower of…" — invites the editor's reflex *this is the textbook construction in geometric clothes* and gives the next four sentences no chance to pre-empt it.

## Checklist (run before circulating any abstract)

1. Couplet present, both halves standard, gap-claim flat.
2. Main theorem in one checkable sentence.
3. One texture clause.
4. One deflation sentence inside the abstract.
5. Concrete closer.
6. Editor test passed against the named venue's editor profile.
7. Rhyme test passed against all sibling island abstracts.
8. Verbs attribution-correct; no praise adjectives; no bullets.
9. Word count within the venue's limit (recorded per venue; JTA 300).
